# Common Edible Wild Plants (Ohio Valley)

**Read [Foraging Safety](/plants/foraging_safety) first.** Confirm every plant with a field guide (Peterson's *Edible Wild Plants*, Samuel Thayer's books) — keep one printed copy.

## Greens

| Plant | Eat | Season | Notes / look-alikes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dandelion** | Leaves (young = less bitter), flowers, roots (roasted = coffee substitute) | Spring–fall | No true look-alikes are dangerous. Cat's ear and chicory are also edible |
| **Broadleaf plantain** | Young leaves (raw/cooked), seeds | Spring–fall | Parallel veins with stringy fibers. Crushed leaves are a classic poultice for stings and scrapes |
| **Chickweed** | Leaves, stems, raw | Cool seasons | Single line of hairs along the stem. Look-alike scarlet pimpernel has no hair line and orange flowers |
| **Lamb's quarters** | Leaves, raw or cooked like spinach; seeds | Summer | Mealy white coating on young leaves. Very nutritious |
| **Purslane** | Leaves and stems | Summer | Succulent, reddish stems. **Look-alike spurge has milky sap**; purslane does not |
| **Wild violet** | Leaves and flowers | Spring | Heart-shaped leaves. Flowers rich in vitamin C |
| **Stinging nettle** | Young tops, **cooked** or dried (removes sting) | Spring | Wear gloves to pick. High protein and iron |
| **Garlic mustard** | Leaves, roots | Fall–spring | Invasive: eat all you want. Garlicky smell when crushed |
| **Wood sorrel** | Leaves, flowers | Spring–fall | Lemony, heart-shaped trifoliate leaves. Small amounts (oxalic acid) |
| **Wild onion / garlic, ramps** | Bulbs and leaves | Spring | **Must smell like onion/garlic.** Ramps grow in rich woods in spring. Harvest sustainably (one leaf per plant) |
| **Cattail** | Young shoots (peeled), green flower spikes (boiled like corn), pollen (flour), rhizome starch | Spring–summer | Wet areas. Look-alikes: irises (toxic) lack the brown "hot dog" seed head and have flat fan-like leaves |

## Fruits & Nuts

| Plant | Eat | Season | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blackberry / black raspberry** | Berries | June–Aug | Aggregate berries. No toxic look-alikes in the region |
| **Wild strawberry** | Berries | May–June | Mock strawberry (yellow flowers) is edible but tasteless |
| **Mulberry** | Ripe (dark) berries | June | Unripe berries can upset stomach |
| **Pawpaw** | Ripe fruit (custard-like, banana-mango flavor) | **Sept–Oct** | Kentucky's native tropical fruit. Don't eat seeds or skin |
| **Persimmon** (American) | Fully ripe, soft fruit | **After first frost**, Sept–Nov | Unripe fruit is horribly astringent |
| **Elderberry** | **Ripe, cooked** purple-black berries; flowers (fritters, tea) | Aug–Sept | **Raw berries, stems, leaves, and unripe berries are toxic.** Cook berries. Look-alikes: water hemlock (different leaves, umbel shape), pokeweed (berries on a stalk, magenta stems) |
| **Wild grapes** | Ripe grapes, young leaves | Late summer–fall | Check for **moonseed** (one crescent seed, smooth leaves, toxic) |
| **Black walnut** | Nutmeats | Fall | Hull stains everything. Cure nuts 2–3 weeks before cracking |
| **Hickory / shagbark** | Nuts | Fall | Boil crushed nuts to separate oil/meat ("hickory milk") |
| **Acorns** | Nutmeats after **leaching** | Fall | Crack, grind, soak/boil in many changes of water until no longer bitter (tannins). White oak acorns need less leaching than red oak |

## Teas & Seasonings
- **Spicebush** — twigs, leaves, berries (allspice substitute)
- **Pine / spruce needle tea** — vitamin C. **Avoid yew** (flat needles, red berries) and avoid if pregnant
- **Sumac (staghorn/smooth)** — red berry clusters steeped cold make "lemonade." **Poison sumac has white berries and grows in swamps**
- **Mint family** — square stems, opposite leaves, minty smell (many species)

## Nutrition Notes
- Wild greens are low-calorie. **Nuts, acorns and cattail starch** provide real calories.
- Wild greens give **vitamins A and C** that a stored-grains diet lacks, which prevents scurvy.
