# Foraging Safety Rules

## The Rules
1. **100% positive ID or don't eat it.** "Probably" kills people.
2. Use **multiple features** — leaves, stem, flowers, smell, root, habitat, season. Not one picture.
3. Know the **deadly look-alikes** for everything you collect.
4. **Start small.** Even safe plants can cause allergic reactions or upset stomachs. Try a small amount and wait 24 hrs.
5. Harvest away from **roadsides, sprayed fields, railroad tracks, contaminated water, and old industrial sites**.
6. Some plants are only edible at **certain stages** or **after cooking**.
7. **Never eat wild mushrooms** unless taught in person by an experienced forager. Field testing doesn't work on mushrooms.

## Deadly Look-Alikes (Eastern US)
| Deadly plant | Mistaken for | Tell-tale signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Water hemlock** (*Cicuta*) — the most toxic plant in North America | Wild carrot, wild parsnip, angelica | Wet areas; **chambered** hollow root/stem base with yellowish oily liquid; veins end at the **notches** between leaf teeth. **Do not touch your mouth after handling.** |
| **Poison hemlock** (*Conium*) | Queen Anne's lace (wild carrot), parsley | **Smooth stem with purple blotches**, no hairs; musty "mousy" smell. Wild carrot has a **hairy** stem and smells like carrot |
| **Death camas** | Wild onion / garlic | **No onion smell.** No smell = don't eat |
| **Pokeweed** (mature) | — | Roots, mature stems, and berries are toxic. Young shoots are traditionally eaten only after **boiling in multiple changes of water**. Not a beginner plant |
| **White snakeroot** | — | Toxic; caused "milk sickness" in pioneer Kentucky via cow milk |
| **Moonseed** | Wild grapes | Moonseed has **one crescent-shaped seed**; grapes have several pear-shaped seeds. Moonseed leaves are smooth-edged |
| **Jimsonweed** | — | All parts toxic; spiny seed pods |
| **Yew** | — | Evergreen with red berries; seeds and foliage deadly |
| Destroying angel / Death cap mushrooms | Button mushrooms, puffballs | White gills, ring, cup (volva) at base. **Slice puffballs: must be pure white inside with no outline of a cap** |

## The "Universal Edibility Test" — Last Resort Only
From U.S. Army FM 21-76/3-05.70. Testing takes **24+ hours per plant part**: skin contact → lip → tongue → chew → swallow tiny amount, waiting hours between steps for any reaction.

**Its limits:** it **will not reliably detect** many deadly toxins (water hemlock, death camas, mushroom amatoxins can take hours to days to show symptoms, by which time damage is done). Use it only if starvation is imminent and you **cannot** identify any known edible. **Learning 10 common plants well is far safer.**

## Plants to Avoid Entirely as a Beginner
- Anything with **milky sap** (except dandelion, wild lettuce, and a few known ones)
- **Umbrella-shaped flower clusters** (carrot family): contains the hemlocks
- Beans/peas in pods (many toxic wild legumes)
- Bitter or soapy taste
- Almond smell from crushed leaves (cyanide compounds, e.g. wild cherry leaves)
- **Three-leaved** plants that could be poison ivy

## Poison Ivy
"Leaves of three, let it be." Hairy vines on trees ("hairy rope, don't be a dope"). Wash skin with soap and cool water within 30 min of contact. Wash clothing and tools. **Never burn it**: inhaled smoke is dangerous.
