# Survival Gardening & Seed Saving

A survival garden grows **calories**, not just salad. Start now — gardening skill takes seasons to build, and soil takes years.

## Kentucky / Ohio Valley Basics
- **USDA zones 6b–7a**
- **Average last spring frost:** mid–late April (Lexington ~Apr 15–20; northern KY/Cincinnati ~Apr 20–25)
- **Average first fall frost:** mid–late October
- **Growing season:** ~180 days

*Local dates vary — use the earlier date to be safe.*

## Calorie Crops (best yield per square foot)
| Crop | Why | Planting |
|---|---|---|
| **Potatoes** | Most calories per area; stores months | Plant seed pieces mid-March–April, 2–4 weeks before last frost |
| **Sweet potatoes** | Huge yields in heat, stores 6+ months, leaves edible | Slips after soil warms, mid-May–June |
| **Winter squash / pumpkins** | Stores 3–6 months, seeds edible | After last frost, May–June |
| **Dry beans** | Protein, stores for years, fixes nitrogen | After last frost, May–June |
| **Field corn / flint / dent** | Stores dry for years, grind for meal | After soil reaches 60°F, May |
| **Sunflowers** | Oil-rich seeds | After last frost |
| **Peanuts** | Protein + fat; grows in KY's season | May, sandy loose soil |
| **Kale / collards** | Nutrients through winter; cold hardy | Early spring and late summer |

## The Three Sisters
A traditional Native American polyculture:
- **Corn** provides a pole,
- **Pole beans** climb it and fix nitrogen,
- **Squash** shades the ground, holding moisture and blocking weeds.
Plant corn in mounds (4–6 per mound, mounds ~4 ft apart). When corn is ~6 in tall, plant beans around it. A week later, plant squash between mounds.

## Planting Calendar (Ohio Valley, approximate)
| When | Plant |
|---|---|
| Feb–Mar (indoors) | Start tomatoes, peppers, cabbage family |
| **Mid March** | Peas, spinach, lettuce, onions, potatoes, kale |
| **April** | Carrots, beets, radishes, cabbage & broccoli transplants |
| **After last frost (late Apr–May)** | Tomatoes, peppers, beans, corn, squash, cucumbers, melons |
| **Late May–June** | Sweet potatoes, okra, Southern peas |
| **Late July–Aug** | Fall crops: kale, collards, turnips, carrots, cabbage, lettuce |

## Seed Saving
- **Save from open-pollinated / heirloom varieties.** Hybrid (F1) seeds don't grow true to type.
- **Easiest for beginners** (self-pollinating): **beans, peas, tomatoes, lettuce, peppers**.
- Harder (cross-pollinate; need isolation): squash, corn, cucumbers, melons, brassicas, onions, carrots.
- **Corn** needs ~200+ plants to avoid inbreeding depression and isolation (~ ¼ mile) from other corn.
- **Squash** of the same species cross. Grow one variety per species or hand-pollinate and bag flowers.

### How
| Crop | Method |
|---|---|
| Beans & peas | Let pods dry brown on the plant; shell, dry another 2 weeks |
| Tomatoes | Scoop seeds with gel, ferment in a jar of water 3–4 days (skim mold), rinse, dry on a plate |
| Peppers | Take seeds from fully ripe (red) fruit, dry |
| Squash | From fully mature fruit (store fruit an extra 3–4 weeks), wash and dry seeds |
| Lettuce | Let bolt, collect fluffy seed heads when dry |

### Storing Seeds
- **Cool, dry, dark.** Paper envelopes inside an airtight jar with a desiccant packet (or dry rice). Fridge is good.
- Label variety + year.
- Viability (approx): onions/parsnips 1–2 yrs · corn, peppers 2–3 · beans, peas 3–4 · tomatoes, squash, cucumbers 4–6+.
- Test germination: 10 seeds in a damp paper towel in a bag; count sprouts after a week.

## Soil & Water
- **Compost everything:** kitchen scraps, leaves, manure (aged), garden waste.
- Mulch heavily (straw, leaves, wood chips) to hold water and block weeds.
- Rainwater catchment for irrigation (see [Finding Water](/water/finding_water)).
- Rotate crop families each year to reduce pests and disease.

## Grid-Down Garden Security
Gardens near the house are easier to protect. Mix food plants into landscaping ("edible landscaping"). Potatoes and root crops are less visible than ripe tomatoes.
