# CPR — Adult, Child & Infant

*Based on American Heart Association / Red Cross guidelines. Take a hands-on class — it matters.*

## Step 1: Check
- Make sure the scene is **safe**.
- Tap and shout: "*Are you OK?*"
- Check for **normal breathing** for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is **not** normal breathing.

## Step 2: Call
- Send someone to call 911 (if any phone works) and to **get an AED**.
- Alone with an adult: call first, then start CPR.
- Alone with a child/infant who collapsed unwitnessed: do **2 minutes of CPR first**, then call.

## Step 3: Compress

| | Adult & teen | Child (1 yr–puberty) | Infant (< 1 yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand position | Heel of hand, center of chest (lower half of breastbone), other hand on top | One or two hands, center of chest | **Two fingers** or two thumbs, just below nipple line |
| Depth | **At least 2 in (5 cm)**, not more than 2.4 in | About 2 in (⅓ chest depth) | About 1.5 in (4 cm) (⅓ chest depth) |
| Rate | **100–120 per minute** | 100–120 | 100–120 |
| Ratio (1 rescuer) | **30 compressions : 2 breaths** | 30 : 2 | 30 : 2 |
| Ratio (2 rescuers) | 30 : 2 | **15 : 2** | **15 : 2** |

- Let the chest **fully recoil** between compressions.
- Rate = the beat of "*Stayin' Alive*."
- Minimize pauses — under 10 seconds for breaths.
- **Untrained or unwilling to give breaths? Do hands-only CPR** (continuous compressions) for adults.

## Step 4: Breathe (if trained)
1. Head-tilt, chin-lift (infant: neutral "sniffing" position).
2. Pinch nose, seal mouth (infant: cover mouth **and** nose).
3. Give 2 breaths, **1 second each**, just enough to make the chest rise.

## Step 5: AED — As Soon As It Arrives
1. Turn it on and **follow the voice prompts**.
2. Bare, dry chest. Pads: upper right chest and lower left side.
3. Everyone **clear** while it analyzes and shocks.
4. Resume compressions immediately after the shock.

## Special Cases
- **Drowning:** start with **2 rescue breaths** (oxygen is the problem), then 30:2.
- **Opioid overdose suspected:** give **naloxone** if available, then CPR.
- **Pregnant:** push the belly gently to her left while doing compressions.

## When to Stop
- The person starts breathing normally or moving
- An AED or trained help takes over
- You are physically exhausted and cannot continue
- The scene becomes unsafe

## Grid-Down Reality
Without EMS and hospitals, survival after cardiac arrest is very low. CPR is still the only chance — but know that **prevention** (managing heart conditions, keeping medications stocked) is the real priority.

## Priority
- **Hard and fast in the center of the chest. Don't stop.**
