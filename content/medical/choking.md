# Choking

## Recognize It
- Can't speak, cough, or breathe; hands at the throat (universal sign)
- Weak, ineffective cough or high-pitched noise
- Lips/face turning blue

**If they can cough forcefully, let them cough.** Stay with them.

## Adult & Child (over 1 year)

1. Ask: "*Are you choking? I'm going to help.*"
2. **5 back blows** — lean them forward, strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. **5 abdominal thrusts** (Heimlich) — stand behind, fist just **above the navel** (thumb side in), grab with other hand, thrust **in and up**.
4. Alternate 5 and 5 until the object comes out or they become unresponsive.

*(The AHA teaches abdominal thrusts alone; the Red Cross teaches the 5-and-5 cycle. Both are acceptable.)*

### Pregnant or very large person
Use **chest thrusts** — hands on the center of the breastbone, pull straight back.

### Choking alone
Thrust your abdomen against the back of a chair, counter edge, or railing — or use your own fist.

## Infant (under 1 year)

1. Lay the infant **face down along your forearm**, head lower than chest, supporting the jaw.
2. **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Turn face up, head lower than chest.
4. **5 chest thrusts** — two fingers just below the nipple line, 1.5 in deep.
5. Repeat until the object comes out or the infant becomes unresponsive.

**Never** do blind finger sweeps — you can push the object deeper. Remove it only if you can **see** it.

## If They Become Unresponsive
1. Lower them to the ground.
2. Start **CPR** ([CPR guide](/medical/cpr_adult)), beginning with compressions.
3. Each time you open the airway to give breaths, **look** in the mouth and remove any visible object.

## Afterward
Anyone who received abdominal thrusts should be checked by a medical provider if possible — internal injury is possible.
