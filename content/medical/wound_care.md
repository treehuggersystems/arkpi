# Wound Cleaning, Closure & Infection

In a grid-down world, **infection** is the big killer from minor wounds. Good cleaning is the most important step.

## 1. Stop the Bleeding
Direct pressure. See [Bleeding Control](/medical/bleeding_control) for serious bleeding.

## 2. Clean — Irrigate, Irrigate, Irrigate
- Wash your hands; wear gloves if you have them.
- **Irrigate with lots of clean water.** Drinking-quality water is fine. Use at least **1 liter**, more for dirty wounds.
- Use **pressure**: a syringe, a squeeze bottle, or a clean zip-lock bag with a pinhole.
- Remove visible dirt and debris with clean tweezers.
- Wash the **skin around** the wound with soap and water. Avoid putting harsh chemicals (hydrogen peroxide, alcohol, full-strength iodine) *into* the wound repeatedly, as they damage tissue.

## 3. Close It — Or Leave It Open?

| Close (tape strips / butterflies) | **Leave open** and pack loosely |
|---|---|
| Clean, straight cut | **Animal or human bites** |
| Less than ~6–8 hrs old (face up to ~24 hrs) | Puncture wounds |
| Edges come together easily | Dirty, crushed or ragged wounds |
| | Wounds older than 8–12 hrs |
| | Any wound you couldn't clean well |

Closing a contaminated wound traps bacteria and can cause a dangerous abscess. Wounds left open heal from the bottom up and scar more, but they are much safer.

**Tape closure:** dry the skin, apply strips across the wound at ~⅛ in intervals, add strips parallel to the wound across their ends to anchor.

## 4. Dress It
- Antibiotic ointment (or honey), then a non-stick pad, then gauze and tape.
- **Change daily** or whenever wet/dirty. Re-irrigate each change.
- Keep elevated for the first day.

## Signs of Infection

| Local (watch closely) | Spreading (serious) |
|---|---|
| Increasing redness, warmth, swelling | **Red streaks** running from the wound |
| Increasing pain after day 2 | **Fever, chills** |
| Pus, foul smell | Rapidly spreading redness (mark the edge with a pen to track) |
| | Confusion, fast heart rate: possible **sepsis** |

### What to do
- Open the wound (remove tape strips), drain pus, irrigate, pack loosely with moist gauze, change twice daily.
- Warm compresses 15 min, 4×/day.
- Spreading infection needs **antibiotics**. Use [MDWiki / WikEM](/library#wiki) for drug references, and the physician's judgement whenever you can reach one.

## Tetanus
Tetanus lives in soil and rust. **Keep tetanus shots current (every 10 years; 5 years for dirty wounds)**. There is no field treatment.

## Priority
- **Irrigate with lots of clean water. If in doubt, leave it open.**
