# Fractures, Sprains & Splinting

## Signs of a Fracture
- Deformity, angulation, or shortening of a limb
- Severe pain at one spot, swelling, bruising
- Can't use or bear weight on it
- Grating sensation (don't test for it)
- **Open fracture:** bone visible or a wound over the break

**When in doubt, treat it as a fracture.**

## Check CSM Before and After Splinting
- **C**irculation — pulse below the injury, skin color/temperature, capillary refill < 2 s
- **S**ensation — can they feel you touch their fingers/toes?
- **M**otion — can they wiggle fingers/toes?

If CSM gets worse after splinting, **loosen it**.

## Splinting Rules
1. **Control bleeding** first. Cover open fractures with a clean moist dressing — don't push bone back in.
2. **Splint in the position found**, unless there's no pulse below the injury and help is hours/days away. Then apply gentle, steady **traction in line** to straighten it, and stop if pain or resistance increases sharply.
3. Immobilize the **joint above and the joint below** a broken bone, or the **bones above and below** an injured joint.
4. **Pad** all hollows and bony points.
5. Tie firmly but **not tight**. You should be able to slip a finger under the ties.
6. Elevate and apply cold packs (20 min on / 20 off) to reduce swelling.

## Improvised Splint Materials
- **Rigid:** boards, sticks, rolled magazines/newspaper, trekking poles, SAM splint, cardboard
- **Padding:** clothing, towels, foam sleeping pad
- **Ties:** cravats, belts, strips of cloth, duct tape (over clothing)
- **Body splint:** tape a finger to its neighbor (buddy tape); tie an injured leg to the good leg

## Common Splints
| Injury | Splint |
|---|---|
| Finger | Buddy-tape to adjacent finger with padding between |
| Wrist / forearm | Rigid splint from palm to elbow, hand in "holding a can" position, then sling |
| Upper arm / shoulder / collarbone | Sling + swathe (bind arm to body) |
| Ankle | Pillow or rolled blanket wrapped around and tied, or SAM splint "stirrup" |
| Lower leg | Rigid splints both sides from thigh to foot |
| Thigh (femur) | Long splint from armpit to foot on outside, groin to foot inside. A femur fracture can lose 1–1.5 L of blood: **watch for shock** |

## Sprains (RICE)
**R**est · **I**ce (20 min at a time, cloth barrier) · **C**ompression (elastic wrap, not tight) · **E**levation above heart. After 48–72 hrs, gentle movement as pain allows.

## Spine Injury Suspected?
Fall from height, diving, high-speed crash, neck/back pain, numbness or tingling:
- Keep head and neck **in line**. Don't move them unless the scene is dangerous.
- If you must move them, **log-roll** with several helpers keeping the spine straight.

## Dislocations
Grid-down, with no medical help coming, some dislocations (shoulder, finger, kneecap) are often reduced in the field. Learn the techniques in **FM 4-25.11** (in the [Library](/library)) and WikEM (Kiwix). Check CSM before and after.

## Priority
- **Pad, splint above and below, check circulation.**
