# Burns

## Step 1: Stop the Burning
- **Stop, drop, and roll** if clothing is on fire; smother flames.
- Remove the person from the heat source — **don't become a second victim**.
- Electrical burn: **turn off power first** before touching them.

## Step 2: Cool — 20 Minutes
- Run **cool (not ice-cold) clean water** over the burn for **20 minutes**. It still helps up to 3 hours after the injury.
- **No ice, no butter, no oil, no toothpaste.** These worsen tissue damage or trap heat.
- Remove rings, watches, belts and tight clothing **before swelling starts**. Don't pull off clothing stuck to the burn.
- Keep the rest of the person **warm** — large burns cause hypothermia fast.

## Step 3: Cover
- Cover loosely with **plastic wrap** (lay it on, don't wrap tightly around a limb), a clean non-stick dressing, or a clean sheet.
- **Do not pop blisters.**

## How Bad Is It?

| Depth | Looks like | Pain | Heals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superficial (1st°) | Red, dry, no blisters (sunburn) | Painful | 3–6 days |
| Partial thickness (2nd°) | Red, **blisters**, moist, blanches | **Very painful** | 2–3 weeks; deep ones scar |
| Full thickness (3rd°) | White, leathery, brown or charred; dry | **Little or no pain** (nerves destroyed) | Needs surgery/grafting |

### Size — Rule of Nines (adult)
| Area | % of body |
|---|---|
| Head & neck | 9% |
| Each arm | 9% |
| Front of torso | 18% |
| Back of torso | 18% |
| Each leg | 18% |
| Groin | 1% |

**Quick estimate:** the patient's **palm (with fingers) ≈ 1%** of their body.

## Serious Burns — Get Advanced Care If At All Possible
- Partial thickness > **10%** of body (> 5% in children/elderly), or any full-thickness burn
- Burns to **face, hands, feet, genitals, or major joints**
- Burns that go **all the way around** a limb or the chest
- Electrical, chemical, or **inhalation** injury: singed nose hairs, soot around mouth, hoarse voice, coughing. **Airway swelling can kill within hours.**

## Chemical Burns
- Brush off dry chemicals first, then flush with **large amounts of water for 20+ minutes**.
- Lye/alkali (see [Lye Safety](/homestead/lye_safety)) penetrates deeply — **flush longer**.
- Eyes: flush continuously for 20 minutes, holding the eyelids open.

## When Help Is Far Away
- **Fluids:** large burns leak huge amounts of fluid. If the person is awake and can swallow, give frequent sips of [oral rehydration solution](/medical/oral_rehydration).
- **Infection** is the main later danger: keep dressings clean, change daily, wash gently with clean water. Watch for spreading redness, pus, foul smell, fever.
- **Pain:** ibuprofen or acetaminophen.
- Honey (medical-grade preferred) has a long history as a burn dressing and has antibacterial properties.

## Priority
- **Cool water, 20 minutes, then cover loosely.** Watch the airway in face/smoke burns.
