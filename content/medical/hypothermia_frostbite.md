# Hypothermia & Frostbite

## Hypothermia

Core temperature below 95°F (35°C). It happens **well above freezing**, especially when wet and windy. 50°F rain is a classic killer.

### Stages

| Stage | Core temp | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | 90–95°F (32–35°C) | **Shivering**, the "umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles; poor judgment |
| Moderate | 82–90°F (28–32°C) | Shivering **slows or stops**, confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech |
| Severe | < 82°F (< 28°C) | Unresponsive, very slow pulse/breathing, rigid. May appear dead |

### Treatment
1. **Get out of the wind and wet.** Shelter, insulation **from the ground**.
2. **Remove wet clothing** (cut it off if needed) and replace with dry layers.
3. **Hypothermia wrap ("burrito"):** sleeping bag/blankets inside a tarp or plastic sheet, wrapped completely, head covered, face exposed.
4. **Heat sources** on the **chest, armpits and back** (not directly on skin): hot water bottles, chemical heat packs, warm stones wrapped in cloth.
5. **Warm, sweet drinks** — only if fully alert and able to swallow. **No alcohol.**
6. Mild cases: food + gentle exercise once dry and warmer.

### Severe hypothermia
- **Handle very gently.** Rough movement can trigger a fatal heart rhythm.
- Keep them **horizontal**.
- Check breathing and pulse for a **full minute**.
- *"Not dead until warm and dead."* People have recovered after long periods of severe hypothermia.

## Frostbite

Frozen skin and tissue, usually fingers, toes, nose, ears and cheeks.

| Depth | Signs |
|---|---|
| Frostnip | Pale, numb, tingling. Recovers fully with warming |
| Superficial | White/gray waxy skin, soft underneath. Blisters after rewarming |
| Deep | Hard, wooden feel, white or blue-gray. Blood blisters, may turn black |

### Treatment
1. **Treat hypothermia first.**
2. **Never rub** frostbitten skin, with snow or anything else. Ice crystals shred tissue.
3. **Do not rewarm if it could refreeze.** Thaw-refreeze is far worse than staying frozen. Walk out on frozen feet if you must.
4. Rewarm in **warm water 98.6–102°F (37–39°C)**, comfortable to a normal hand, for 15–30 min until skin is soft and color returns. It will be **very painful**: give ibuprofen.
5. Don't use fires, heaters or car exhaust. Numb skin burns easily.
6. Pad between fingers/toes, loose dry dressings, don't pop blisters, elevate.

## Trench / Immersion Foot
Cold + wet for hours or days (doesn't need freezing). Feet swollen, numb, red then pale. **Prevention:** dry socks daily, air feet out, never sleep in wet socks.

## Prevention — C.O.L.D.
**C**lean clothes · avoid **O**verheating (sweat = wet) · wear **L**ayers loose · stay **D**ry
- Wool and synthetics insulate when wet; **cotton kills**.
- Eat and drink. The body burns calories to stay warm.
- Use the [Wind Chill tool](/tools/weather) for frostbite timing.

## Priority
- **Dry, insulated, rewarm the core. Handle severe cases gently.**
