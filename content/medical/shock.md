# Shock

Shock means the body's organs aren't getting enough blood flow. It's a killer after **bleeding, burns, severe infection, dehydration, or allergic reactions**.

## Signs (early → late)
- Anxiety, restlessness, a feeling of doom
- **Fast pulse**, fast shallow breathing
- **Pale, cool, clammy** skin; gray or blue lips
- Thirst, nausea
- Capillary refill > 2 seconds (press a fingernail — color should return within 2 s)
- Confusion, drowsiness → unresponsive
- Weak or absent wrist pulse (late, ominous sign)

## What to Do
1. **Treat the cause** — [stop the bleeding](/medical/bleeding_control) first; epinephrine for [anaphylaxis](/medical/anaphylaxis).
2. **Lay them flat** on their back.
   - If no leg, hip, head or spine injury, you can raise the legs about 12 in (30 cm).
   - Breathing trouble? Let them sit up. Vomiting? Put them on their side.
3. **Keep them warm** — insulate **underneath** them (ground steals heat) and cover them. Even in summer.
4. **Reassure** them and keep them still.
5. **Nothing by mouth** if you expect surgery or they're confused. If rescue is days away and they're awake and can swallow, small sips of [oral rehydration solution](/medical/oral_rehydration) are reasonable.
6. Monitor breathing, pulse and responsiveness every 5 minutes — use the [vital signs guide](/medical/vital_signs).

## Types (to understand the cause)

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | Blood or fluid loss — bleeding, burns, diarrhea |
| Cardiogenic | Heart attack, heart failure |
| Distributive — septic | Severe infection |
| Distributive — anaphylactic | Allergic reaction |
| Obstructive | Collapsed lung (tension pneumothorax), cardiac tamponade |

## Priority
- **Stop bleeding, lay flat, keep warm.** Shock gets worse quietly — keep checking.
