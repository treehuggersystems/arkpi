# Emergency Shelter & the Rule of 3s

## Survival Priorities — Rule of 3s
You can survive roughly:
- **3 minutes** without air (or with severe bleeding)
- **3 hours** without shelter in harsh weather
- **3 days** without water
- **3 weeks** without food

**Shelter comes before water and food.** Exposure kills faster than thirst.

## Choosing a Site
- **Avoid:** dead trees and hanging branches ("widowmakers"), dry creek beds and low ground (flash floods, cold air pools), hilltops (wind, lightning), ant nests, animal trails.
- **Look for:** level, well-drained ground; natural windbreaks; nearby materials, water, firewood; some visibility for signaling.
- Mid-slope is often warmest (cold air sinks to valley floors at night).

## Insulate From the Ground First
The ground steals heat **far faster** than air. Make a bed **6–12 in thick** (compressed) of dry leaves, pine needles, grass, spruce boughs, or a foam pad. This matters more than the roof.

## Tarp Shelters
A 8×10 ft tarp + 50 ft of cordage covers most needs. See [Knots](/shelter/knots) for the ridgeline and tensioning knots.

| Setup | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **A-frame** | Ridgeline between two trees, tarp draped over, edges staked | Rain from all directions; simple |
| **Lean-to** | One edge tied high to a ridgeline, other edge staked to ground, back to the wind | Fire reflection, good views, dry weather |
| **Plow point / diamond** | One corner high on a tree, opposite corner and sides staked | One tree, wind and rain |
| **Tube tent / burrito** | Tarp under and over you | Emergency, minimal |

**Ridgeline:** run it **slightly downhill** so rain doesn't pool, and use a drip line (string hanging off the ridgeline) to keep water from running down the cord into your shelter.

## Debris Hut (no gear)
1. Find or set a strong **ridgepole** (~9 ft) with one end on a stump/rock at hip height, the other on the ground.
2. Lean **ribbing sticks** along both sides close together, steep enough to shed rain.
3. Pile **leaves/debris 2–3 ft thick** over everything (thicker = warmer). Add branches on top to hold it in wind.
4. Stuff the inside with dry leaves; crawl in feet first. Plug the door with a pile of leaves.
5. Keep it **just big enough for your body**. Small = warm.

## Snow Shelters
- **Quinzhee:** pile snow 6–7 ft high, let it sinter 1–2 hrs, hollow it out to ~1 ft walls (push in 12-in sticks as depth gauges). Ventilation hole in the roof. Door below sleeping level.
- **Snow trench:** dig a body-length trench, roof with branches/tarp, cover with snow.
- Snow insulates: inside stays around 32°F regardless of how cold it is outside.

## Fire & Shelter
- A **reflector wall** (stacked logs or rocks) behind the fire bounces heat into a lean-to.
- Keep fires **away from tarp** material and debris huts.
- See [Fire Starting Basics](/homestead/fire_starting_basics).

## Urban Shelter
- Vehicles, interior rooms, basements, under stairwells.
- Avoid damaged buildings (aftershocks, collapse, gas leaks).
- In heat: underground parking, basements, shade with airflow.
