# Essential Knots

Practice until you can tie these **in the dark with cold hands**. For more, the zimgit "Knots" library is in Kiwix.

## Loops
### Bowline: fixed loop that won't slip or jam
1. Make a small overhand loop in the standing part (the "rabbit hole"), with the working end on top.
2. Bring the working end **up through** the hole (rabbit comes out of the hole),
3. **around behind** the standing part (around the tree),
4. and **back down** through the hole.
5. Dress and pull tight. Leave a long tail.
*Use:* tie around a tree, rescue loop, mooring. Can loosen when not under load, so back it up with a stopper knot for critical uses.

### Figure-Eight on a Bight: strongest, easiest-to-inspect loop
Fold the rope to make a bight, tie a figure-eight with the doubled rope. *Use:* climbing/rescue anchor, clipping in.

## Hitches (rope to object)
### Clove Hitch
Two loops around a post, the second crossing over the first, end tucked under the second wrap. Quick and adjustable but can slip, so add half hitches. *Use:* start lashings, ridgeline ends.

### Two Half Hitches
Pass around the object, wrap the end around the standing part twice in the same direction. *Use:* securing a line to a post, ring, or tree.

### Taut-Line Hitch: adjustable tension
1. Pass the end around the stake.
2. Wrap **twice inside** the loop around the standing part (toward the stake),
3. then **once outside** (beyond the first wraps),
4. snug. Slide to tighten; holds under load.
*Use:* **tent and tarp guylines**.

### Trucker's Hitch: 3:1 mechanical advantage
1. Make a slip-loop (or directional figure-8) mid-line.
2. Run the end around the anchor and back **through the loop**.
3. Pull hard: the loop acts as a pulley.
4. Lock off with **two half hitches** below the loop.
*Use:* **ridgelines**, loads on vehicles, anything that needs to be drum-tight.

### Prusik: friction hitch that slides when loose, grips when loaded
A loop of thinner cord wrapped **3 times** around the main rope, passed through itself. *Use:* adjustable tarp tie-outs on a ridgeline, ascending a rope, hauling.

### Timber Hitch
Wrap around the log, then wrap the end around itself 3–4 times. *Use:* dragging logs, starting a diagonal lashing.

## Bends (joining ropes)
### Sheet Bend: join two ropes, even different sizes
1. Make a bight in the **thicker** rope.
2. Pass the thinner end up through the bight, around **behind both legs** of the bight,
3. and **under itself** (not through the bight).
Double sheet bend: go around twice for more security.

### Square (Reef) Knot: "right over left, left over right"
*Use:* tying bandages, bundles. **Not for joining ropes under load or anything life-critical.** It can capsize and slip.

## Stopper
### Figure-Eight Stopper
Loop the end back, around the standing part, and down through the loop: looks like an "8". Keeps rope from running through a hole or device.

## Cordage Tips
- **Paracord (550):** 550 lb rating, 7 inner strands usable for sewing, fishing, snares.
- Melt cut synthetic ends with a lighter to stop fraying; whip natural rope ends with thread.
- Knots reduce rope strength by 20–50%.
- Natural cordage: inner bark (basswood, cedar), dogbane, nettle fibers, reverse-wrapped into two-ply string.
