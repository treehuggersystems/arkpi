# Staying Warm Without Power

## ⚠ Never Indoors
Charcoal grills, camp stoves, gas ovens/ranges for heat, generators, outdoor propane heaters. **CO kills silently.** Run a battery **CO alarm** in any room with a fuel-burning heater.

## 1. Shrink Your Space
- Pick **one small interior room** (fewest windows, on the sunny side or lowest floor). Everyone sleeps there.
- Close doors to the rest of the house. Hang **blankets over doorways**.
- Set up a **tent indoors** in that room: body heat warms a small space fast.

## 2. Insulate
- Windows lose the most heat: tape **bubble wrap** or plastic sheeting over them; hang heavy blankets/quilts; close curtains at night, **open south-facing ones on sunny days**.
- Roll towels against drafts under doors.
- Rugs and blankets on floors; sit/sleep **off the floor** (cold sinks).

## 3. Safe Heat Sources
| Heater | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Wood stove / fireplace insert** | Best. Fireplace alone loses most heat up the chimney. Clean chimney yearly |
| **Indoor-rated propane heater** (e.g. Mr. Heater Buddy) | Has low-oxygen shutoff (ODS) and tip-over switch. **Still crack a window, still use a CO alarm.** Use only indoor-rated models |
| **Kerosene heater** (indoor-rated) | Use K-1 kerosene only. Ventilate. CO alarm |
| Gas fireplace with standing pilot / no blower | Often works without power. Check your model |
| Gas furnace + generator/inverter | The blower and controls need ~400–800 W |
| Body heat, pets, hot water | Free |

**Candles** give very little heat and are a major fire cause during outages. Use LED lanterns for light.

## 4. Dress & Sleep Warm
- **Layers:** base (wool/synthetic), insulating (fleece/down), shell. **No cotton.** Hat (lots of heat loss from head and neck), dry socks, gloves.
- **Sleep:** zero-degree sleeping bags or stacked blankets **underneath you** as well as on top. Share beds. Wear a hat.
- **Hot water bottle** (or Nalgene bottle with hot water, wrapped in a sock) in the sleeping bag at the feet or between thighs.
- Eat before bed: digesting food produces heat. Fats burn slowly.
- Stay dry: change out of sweaty clothes.

## 5. Keep Pipes From Freezing
Let faucets drip, open cabinets on outside walls, and if the house will freeze for days, [winterize the plumbing](/repair/plumbing_basics).

## Warning Signs
- **Hypothermia** creeps in indoors too, especially for elderly and infants. Watch for shivering, confusion, fumbling: see [Hypothermia](/medical/hypothermia_frostbite).
- **CO poisoning:** headache, dizziness, nausea in several people at once → get outside immediately.

## Firewood
- A cord = 4 × 4 × 8 ft = 128 cu ft. Heating a home through a cold-climate winter commonly takes **3–6+ cords** with a decent stove.
- Seasoned wood (split, stacked, covered, **6–12 months**) burns hot and clean. Green wood smokes and builds creosote.
- Best heat: oak, hickory, ash, locust, maple. Poor: pine (fine for kindling), poplar, willow.
