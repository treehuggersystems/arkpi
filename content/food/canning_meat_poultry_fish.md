# Canning Meat, Poultry, Venison & Fish

Canned meat is one of the most valuable things you can put on a shelf. It's fully cooked, shelf-stable, needs **no freezer**, and is ready to eat. It's also how you save a deer or a freezer full of meat when the power goes out.

**All meat, poultry and fish must be pressure canned.** Use the [Canning Lookup](/tools/canning) for your altitude.

## General Rules
- Use **fresh, chilled** meat, kept below 40°F until canning.
- **Trim fat** and gristle. Fat can creep onto the jar rim and cause seal failure.
- **No flour, breading, or thickeners.** Don't add pasta, rice, or dairy.
- Salt is optional (1 tsp per quart) for flavor.
- Wipe rims with a cloth dampened with **vinegar** to cut grease before placing lids.
- Use the full headspace (1–1¼ in). Meat expands.

## Hot Pack vs Raw Pack
| | Raw pack | Hot pack |
|---|---|---|
| How | Pack raw meat loosely, **add no liquid** (meat makes its own juice) | Brown, roast or simmer until rare/medium, pack hot, cover with boiling broth, water or tomato juice |
| Pros | Fast, simple, rich flavor | Better color, more meat per jar |
| Time | Same processing time either way |

## By Type
### Chicken / Turkey / Rabbit
- Cut into pieces. **Bone-in** processes faster (the bones conduct heat). **Boneless** packs more meat per jar.
- Hot pack: boil, steam or bake until about ⅔ done; pack, cover with hot broth.
- Raw pack: pack pieces loosely, no liquid.

### Beef, Pork, Venison, Lamb (strips, cubes, chunks)
- Cut into 1–2 in cubes or strips. Venison: soak in salt water 1 hr to draw blood if desired; trim **all** fat and silverskin (venison fat tastes strong).
- Raw pack (no liquid) or hot pack after browning.
- Great as stew meat, BBQ, tacos: open, heat, season.

### Ground or Chopped Meat
- Shape into patties or loose crumbles, **brown**, drain fat.
- Pack hot, cover with boiling broth, tomato juice or water.
- Don't add fillers (bread, egg).

### Fish (salmon, trout, etc.)
- Pints or half-pints only; **100 minutes**.
- Raw pack skin-side out; no liquid. The bones become soft and edible (calcium!).

### Stock / Broth
- Simmer bones 3–4 hrs, strain, **chill and skim fat**, reheat to boiling, pack hot. 20/25 min.

## Wild Game Notes
- Field-dress fast and cool carcasses quickly.
- Never can meat from animals that looked sick.
- Bear and wild boar can carry *Trichinella*. Pressure canning kills it, but handle raw meat carefully.

## Yields
- About **2–2½ lb** of boneless raw meat (or 3–4 lb bone-in chicken) per quart.
- A field-dressed deer (~100 lb) yields roughly 40–50 lb of boneless meat ≈ 20–25 quarts.

## Using Canned Meat
- Ready to eat as-is. The USDA recommends boiling home-canned low-acid foods 10 minutes before eating as an extra safety margin if you have **any** doubt about the processing.
- Shelf life: best quality within **1 year**, safe as long as the seal holds and it was processed correctly. See [Storage Life](/food/preserved_food_storage_life).

## Priority
- **Pressure canner only. Trim the fat. Wipe rims with vinegar.**
