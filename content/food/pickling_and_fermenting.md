# Pickling & Fermenting

Two ways to make vegetables acidic enough to keep:
1. **Vinegar pickling:** add acid (vinegar). Fast; shelf-stable after water-bath processing.
2. **Fermentation:** salt lets good bacteria make lactic acid over weeks. Adds probiotics and vitamins; keep cool or can it.

## Ingredients That Matter
- **Vinegar:** must be labeled **5% acidity** (white distilled or cider). Homemade vinegar's strength is unknown, so don't use it for canned pickles.
- **Never reduce** the vinegar or change the vinegar-to-water ratio in a recipe. That's what makes it safe. You **can** add sugar to taste.
- **Salt:** canning/pickling salt (no iodine or anti-caking agents, which darken pickles and cloud brine). Kosher salt without additives works, but measure by weight.
- **Water:** soft water is best; hard water can cloud brine.
- **Cucumbers:** pickling varieties (Kirby), unwaxed, picked **within 24 hours**. Cut **1/16 in off the blossom end**: it contains enzymes that soften pickles.

## Crisp Pickles
- Soak cucumbers in **ice water 4–5 hours** before pickling.
- Add **calcium chloride** (Pickle Crisp): about ⅛ tsp per pint, ¼ tsp per quart.
- **Low-temperature pasteurization** (USDA option for quick dills): process jars in water held at **180–185°F for 30 minutes** (use a thermometer; don't let it exceed 185°F). Firmer pickles, still safe.
- Grape, oak or horseradish leaves (tannins) are a traditional crisping trick.
- Alum and pickling lime are no longer recommended (lime must be thoroughly rinsed if used).

## Quick Refrigerator Pickles (no canning)
Basic brine: **1 cup 5% vinegar + 1 cup water + 1 Tbsp pickling salt** (+ 1–2 Tbsp sugar optional). Bring to a boil, pour over packed vegetables and spices, cool, refrigerate. Ready in 24 hrs; keeps about **1–2 months** in the fridge. Works for cucumbers, onions, carrots, green beans, jalapeños, cauliflower, radishes.

## Fermentation (Lacto-fermentation)
### Sauerkraut
1. Shred **5 lb cabbage** finely; mix with **3 Tbsp canning salt** (about 2–2.5% by weight).
2. Pound or squeeze until it releases juice (10–15 min).
3. Pack tightly into a crock or jars, pressing until **brine covers** the cabbage.
4. Weigh it down (a brine-filled bag, plate + jar of water, or glass weights). **Everything under the brine.**
5. Ferment at **70–75°F: 3–4 weeks**; 60–65°F: 5–6 weeks. Below 60°F it may not ferment; above 75°F it may go soft.
6. Skim any surface scum. **Pink, slimy, or moldy-throughout kraut: discard.**
7. Store tightly covered in the fridge for **several months**, or can it (times above).

### Fermented Vegetables / Dill Pickles
- Brine strength around **3.5–5% salt** (roughly 2–3 Tbsp canning salt per quart of water), with dill, garlic, spices.
- Keep vegetables submerged; ferment 1–4 weeks depending on temperature and vegetable.
- Fermented dills: when they're olive-green throughout and taste sour, refrigerate or can them.

### Signs of Good vs Bad Fermentation
| OK | Discard |
|---|---|
| Bubbles, cloudy brine, sour smell | Fuzzy colored mold on the food itself |
| White film (kahm yeast) on the surface: skim it | Slimy or mushy texture |
| | Rotten / putrid smell, pink color in kraut |

## Relish & Chutney
Chop vegetables (cucumbers, zucchini, peppers, onions, green tomatoes), salt and drain, cook in vinegar and sugar per a tested recipe, pack hot, process **10 min**. A great way to preserve summer squash and zucchini, which can't be plain-canned.

## Pickled Eggs
**Refrigerator only.** There's no tested method for room-temperature storage. Botulism has been linked to home-pickled eggs stored at room temperature. Keep refrigerated and use within 3–4 months.

## Priority
- **5% vinegar, never diluted. Keep ferments under the brine.**
