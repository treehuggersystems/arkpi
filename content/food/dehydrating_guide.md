# Dehydrating & Freeze-Drying

Drying removes the water bacteria and mold need. Dried food is **light, compact, and needs no power to store**, which makes it ideal for go-bags and long-term pantries. Solar and wood-stove drying work grid-down too (see [Preserving Without Power](/food/preserving_without_power)).

## Temperatures
| Food | Dryer temperature |
|---|---|
| Herbs | 95–115°F |
| Vegetables | 125–135°F |
| Fruit & fruit leather | 135°F |
| **Jerky** | Heat meat to **160°F** (poultry **165°F**) first, then dry at 145–155°F |

## Prep & Approximate Times (electric dehydrator)
| Food | Prep | Hours | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apples | Core, slice ¼ in, dip in lemon/ascorbic solution | 6–12 | Leathery, pliable, no moisture when squeezed |
| Bananas | Slice ¼ in, dip | 8–10 | Leathery to crisp |
| Berries (blueberries, cranberries) | Blanch 30–60 s to crack skins | 10–20 | Shriveled, leathery |
| Peaches, pears, apricots | Halve or slice, dip | 10–20 | Leathery |
| Strawberries | Slice ½ in | 7–15 | Leathery to crisp |
| Tomatoes | Slice ¼ in or halve small ones | 6–12 | Leathery to crisp |
| Carrots | Slice ⅛ in, blanch 3 min | 6–10 | Brittle |
| Corn | Blanch, cut kernels | 6–10 | Brittle |
| Green beans | Cut, blanch 2 min | 8–14 | Brittle |
| Onions | Slice ¼ in, no blanch | 6–10 | Crisp (dry outdoors or in garage: strong smell) |
| Peppers | Slice/dice, no blanch | 8–12 | Leathery to brittle |
| Potatoes | Slice ⅛ in or shred, cook until just tender | 8–12 | Brittle |
| Mushrooms (store-bought) | Slice ¼ in | 4–10 | Brittle |
| Herbs | Stems on, rinse, pat dry | 1–4 | Crumble easily |
| Fruit leather | Purée fruit (+ honey optional), spread ⅛ in on lined tray | 6–8 | No sticky spots, peels off |

*Times vary with humidity, thickness, and load. Rotate trays.*

## Jerky
1. Use **lean** meat: beef round, venison, turkey breast. Fat goes rancid.
2. Partially freeze, slice **¼ in** thick, against the grain for tender, with the grain for chewy.
3. Marinate in the fridge (salt/soy sauce, spices) up to 24 hrs.
4. **Heat to 160°F** (165°F poultry) before drying: steam or roast in the marinade, or heat in a 275°F oven after drying until it reaches temperature. Drying alone at low temps may not kill *E. coli* or *Salmonella*.
5. Dry at 145–155°F until it bends and cracks but doesn't break: 4–6 hrs after heating.
6. Storage: about **1–2 months** in an airtight container at room temperature; longer in the fridge or freezer.

## Conditioning Dried Fruit
Moisture is uneven after drying. Before storing:
1. Pack cooled fruit loosely in glass jars, ⅔ full, and seal.
2. Shake daily for **7–10 days**.
3. Condensation inside the jar means it isn't dry enough: return it to the dryer.

## Storage
- Airtight glass jars, Mylar with an oxygen absorber, or vacuum bags. Cool, dark, dry.
- **Dried fruit:** about **1 year at 60°F**, 6 months at 80°F.
- **Dried vegetables:** about **half** that (6 months at 60°F). Vegetables have less sugar and acid.
- Check periodically for moisture or mold.
- Very dry ("brittle") foods in Mylar with O₂ absorbers last much longer.

## Rehydrating
- Soak in water: vegetables 1–2 hrs (or simmer 20–30 min); fruit 8 hrs or overnight.
- 1 cup dried vegetables ≈ 2 cups rehydrated.
- Add dried vegetables straight into soups and stews.

## Home Freeze-Drying
Home freeze dryers (e.g., Harvest Right) freeze food and then remove ice under vacuum.
- Keeps flavor, color, nutrition and shape far better than dehydrating; works for **full meals, dairy, eggs, meat, fruit**.
- Packed in **Mylar with an oxygen absorber**, low-fat freeze-dried foods can last **up to 25 years**. High-fat foods (bacon, nuts, butter) won't last as long.
- Downsides: expensive, cycles take 20–40+ hours, and it needs substantial power, so it's not a grid-down tool. Run it now to build up stores.
