# How Long Preserved Food Lasts

Track your stock in the [Pantry Inventory](/tools/pantry). Shelf life for bulk dry goods (rice, beans, wheat) is in [Food Storage & Shelf Life](/food/food_shelf_life).

## Master Chart
| Food | Best quality within | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Home-canned (all types)** | **1 year** (USDA) | Safe longer if properly processed and the seal holds, but color, texture and nutrition decline. Eat oldest first |
| Commercial canned, high-acid (tomatoes, fruit, juice) | 12–18 months | Acid slowly attacks the can |
| Commercial canned, low-acid (meat, vegetables, soup) | 2–5 years | Often edible much longer if the can is perfect |
| Home-canned pickles, relish, jam | 1 year | Jam may darken; still OK if sealed |
| Frozen meat (steaks, roasts) | 4–12 months | Safe indefinitely at 0°F |
| Frozen vegetables & fruit | 8–12 months | Blanch vegetables first |
| Dried fruit | ~1 year at 60°F · 6 months at 80°F | Airtight, dark |
| Dried vegetables | ~6 months at 60°F | Brittle-dry in Mylar lasts longer |
| Jerky (properly heated) | 1–2 months room temp | Longer in fridge/freezer |
| Freeze-dried (Mylar + O₂ absorber) | Up to 25 years | Low-fat foods |
| Sauerkraut, fermented veg (refrigerated) | Several months | Or water-bath can |
| Refrigerator pickles | 1–2 months | Not shelf-stable |
| Pickled eggs | 3–4 months | **Refrigerated only** |
| Root vegetables in a cellar | 3–8 months | See [root cellaring table](/food/preserving_without_power) |
| Whole uncut dry-cured country ham | ~1 year at room temp | Once cut, refrigerate |
| Eggs in shell, refrigerated | 3–5 weeks | Unwashed farm eggs keep a few weeks unrefrigerated (bloom intact) |
| Water-glassed eggs (pickling lime) | 8–12+ months | Fresh, clean, unwashed eggs only |
| Rendered lard / tallow | Months in a cool dark place | Freeze for a year+ |
| Honey, sugar, salt | Indefinite | Keep dry. Crystallized honey: warm gently |

## Storing Home-Canned Jars
- **Remove the rings** after jars cool 12–24 hrs. Rings can rust and hide a failed seal. Wash jar exteriors.
- **Label:** contents + date (+ batch if you had problems).
- **Cool, dark, dry: 50–70°F.** Heat speeds quality loss; light fades color.
- **Don't let jars freeze.** Freezing can break seals and jars.
- Don't stack jars directly on each other. If you must, add a sheet of cardboard and no more than two layers.
- Keep away from furnaces, water heaters, hot pipes, and direct sun.

## Spoilage: Check Every Jar Before Opening
**Do not taste food you suspect is spoiled.**
- Lid **bulging**, unsealed, or leaking
- Liquid **spurts** when opened; foam or bubbles rising
- **Cloudy** liquid that wasn't cloudy when canned, or sediment that isn't starch
- **Mold** (on food or under the lid)
- **Off smell**, unnatural color, slimy or mushy texture

### If you find a spoiled or suspect low-acid jar
1. **Don't open it** if it's swollen. Bag it, seal, and put it in the trash where children and animals can't reach it.
2. If it's open or leaking: wear gloves, put jar and lid in a pot, cover with water, **boil 30 minutes** (detoxifies botulinum toxin), then discard.
3. Clean every surface it touched with **1 part bleach to 5 parts water**, wait 30 min, rinse.
4. Wash hands thoroughly.

## Extra Safety Margin
USDA: if you're unsure a low-acid home-canned food was processed correctly, **boil it for 10 minutes** (plus 1 minute per 1,000 ft altitude) before eating. Botulinum toxin is destroyed by boiling.

## Botulism Symptoms
12–36 hours after eating (range: hours to days): double/blurred vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, trouble swallowing, dry mouth, muscle weakness moving down the body, difficulty breathing. **This is a medical emergency.** Seek antitoxin and supportive care immediately if any medical help exists.
