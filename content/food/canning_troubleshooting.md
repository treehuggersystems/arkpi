# Canning Troubleshooting

## Jar Didn't Seal
Press the center of the lid after 12–24 hrs. If it flexes or pops, it's not sealed.
- **Within 24 hours:** reprocess. Remove the lid, check the rim for nicks, use a **new lid**, and process for the **full time** again (quality will suffer a bit).
- Or **refrigerate** and eat within a few days, or **freeze** (leave headspace).
- **Common causes:** food or grease on the rim, nicked rim, rings too loose or too tight, not enough headspace, under-processing, old or reused flat lids.

## Liquid Lost From Jars (Siphoning)
Jars sealed but liquid level dropped.
- **Safe** if sealed. Food above the liquid may darken; use those jars first.
- **Causes:** rapid pressure or temperature changes (pressure canner not cooled naturally, or lid removed too soon), not removing air bubbles, overfilling, fluctuating canner pressure, starchy foods absorbing liquid.
- **Fix:** let the canner depressurize on its own; wait 10 min after opening before lifting jars; keep pressure steady; remove bubbles with a plastic tool.

## Cloudy Liquid
- **Starch** from potatoes, beans, corn or overripe vegetables, **hard water minerals**, or **table salt** additives cause harmless cloudiness.
- **Spoilage** also causes cloudiness. If the lid is bulging, there's an off smell, bubbles, or spurting: **discard** (see [Storage Life](/food/preserved_food_storage_life) for safe disposal).
- **If in doubt, throw it out.**

## Fruit Floats
- Fruit is lighter than syrup, especially in raw packs or heavy syrup.
- **Fix:** hot pack, use ripe but firm fruit, pack tightly, use a lighter syrup.
- Safe to eat.

## Food Darkens at the Top
- Too much headspace (air), not enough liquid, under-processing, or air bubbles left in.
- If sealed and processed properly, it's safe; quality is lower.

## Jars Break in the Canner
- **Thermal shock:** hot food into cold jars, cold jars into boiling water, or cold air on hot jars.
- Jars set directly on the canner bottom (**always use the rack**).
- Hairline cracks or old, overused jars; commercial "one-trip" mayo jars.
- **Fix:** keep jars hot until filling, use the rack, inspect jars, don't put lids/rings on too tight.

## Lid Buckled or Bent
Rings tightened too much. Air couldn't escape. **Fingertip tight** only.

## Black / Brown Deposits Under the Lid
Natural compounds in some foods (corn, broccoli, onions) react with the metal. **Harmless** if the jar is sealed and food looks and smells normal.

## Pink, Blue, or Green Garlic / Onions / Cauliflower
Natural pigment reactions with acid or metals. **Harmless.**

## Soft or Hollow Pickles
- Soft: blossom ends not removed, vinegar too weak, too little salt, over-processed, or old cucumbers.
- Hollow: cucumbers left too long before pickling, or growing problems.
- **Slimy** pickles are spoiled: discard.

## Jam Didn't Set
- Wait 24–48 hrs; some take time.
- Remake: per the pectin maker's instructions for re-cooking, or use it as syrup or ice-cream topping.
- Causes: inaccurate measuring, doubled batches, reduced sugar with regular pectin, undercooking.

## Canner Problems
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Steam leaking around lid | Replace the **gasket**; check the lid is locked |
| Pressure won't build | Not venting 10 min first, gasket worn, vent pipe blocked (clean with pipe cleaner), heat too low |
| Overpressure plug blew | Replace it; never run without one. Pressure was too high or vent was blocked |
| Dial gauge accuracy | Test yearly (county Extension office); replace if more than 2 lb off |
| Weighted gauge rocking | Should rock 1–4 times per minute (or as manual says); if it rocks constantly, heat is too high |

## Priority
- **Unsealed → reprocess within 24 hrs, refrigerate, or freeze. Suspect spoilage → never taste it.**
