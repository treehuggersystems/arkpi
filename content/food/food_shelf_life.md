# Food Storage & Shelf Life

Track what you have in the [Pantry Inventory](/tools/pantry) and size your stores with the [Food Storage Planner](/tools/food).

## The Enemies of Stored Food
**H**eat · **O**xygen · **L**ight · **M**oisture · **P**ests
- Every **18°F (10°C) cooler roughly doubles** shelf life. Aim for **below 70°F**, ideally 40–60°F.
- Basements are good, attics and garages are bad.

## Shelf Life Table

| Food | Retail packaging | Sealed long-term (Mylar + O₂ absorber, cool) |
|---|---|---|
| **White rice** | 2 years | **25–30 years** |
| Brown rice | 6 months (oils go rancid) | Not recommended |
| **Hard wheat berries** | Years | **25–30 years** |
| White flour | 6–12 months | ~5 years |
| Whole wheat flour | 1–3 months (freeze) | Not recommended. Store berries + grain mill |
| Rolled oats | 1 year | 25–30 years |
| **Dry beans / lentils** | 1–2 years (harder to cook) | **25–30 years** |
| Pasta | 2 years | 20–30 years |
| Cornmeal (degermed) | 1 year | 5–10 years |
| Popcorn (whole) | 2 years | 10+ years. Also grind for cornmeal |
| **Sugar, salt, honey** | **Indefinite** (keep dry) | Indefinite |
| Nonfat dry milk | 1–2 years | ~20 years |
| Baking soda | Indefinite (keep dry) | Indefinite |
| Baking powder | 6–18 months | Loses potency |
| Instant yeast (unopened) | 1–2 years; freeze to extend | Keep cold. Also maintain a sourdough starter |
| Vegetable oil | 1–2 years | Rotate. Doesn't store long |
| Peanut butter | 6–24 months | Rotate |
| Canned meats & veg (low-acid) | **2–5 years** | Often safe much longer if can is perfect |
| Canned fruit & tomatoes (high-acid) | 12–18 months | Acid eats cans. Rotate |
| Freeze-dried meals (#10 cans) | 25 years | — |
| MREs | ~5 years at 80°F; 3 at 90°F | Heat kills them fast |
| Home-canned foods | 1 year best quality | Safe as long as seal holds and properly processed |
| Spices (whole) | 3–4 years | Ground loses potency in 1–2 |
| Coffee (whole bean, sealed) | 1 year | 2+ years. Instant lasts longer |

## Packing Bulk Staples (Mylar Method)
1. **Mylar bag** (5–7 mil) inside a **food-grade 5-gal bucket**.
2. Fill with dry food (< 10% moisture — rice, wheat, beans, oats, pasta).
3. Add **oxygen absorbers**: about **2,000 cc per 5-gal bucket**. Work fast; open absorbers go dead in ~15 min.
4. Press out air, **heat-seal** with an iron or hair straightener on a board.
5. Lid the bucket, **label contents + date**.
6. **Never** seal sugar or salt with O₂ absorbers. They turn rock hard.
7. **Never** store moist foods (>10% moisture) in low-oxygen packaging: **botulism risk**.

## Can Safety: Discard If
- **Bulging**, swollen, or leaking
- Deep dents on the **seam** or rim, or sharp dents
- Rust that doesn't wipe off / pits through
- Spurts liquid or foams when opened, or smells off

## Rotation
- **First in, first out.** Put new stock in the back.
- "Store what you eat, eat what you store." The best food storage is your normal groceries ×3 months.
- Keep a **manual can opener** (two).

## Don't Forget
- Multivitamins, vitamin C
- Comfort foods and spices: appetite fatigue is real
- Infant formula / special diets / pet food
- Cooking fuel (see [Cooking Without Power](/food/cooking_without_power))
