# Preserving Food Without Power

## When the Power Goes Out
| | Stays safe (door closed) |
|---|---|
| Refrigerator | **~4 hours** |
| Full freezer | **~48 hours** |
| Half-full freezer | ~24 hours |

- **Keep doors shut.** Put a thermometer inside.
- USDA rule: discard perishables (meat, dairy, leftovers, eggs) held **above 40°F for more than 2 hours**.
- Thawed food that still has **ice crystals or is ≤ 40°F can be refrozen**.
- Winter: a cooler outside in the shade works as a fridge. Watch for animals and freezing.
- Plan: eat fridge food first, then freezer food, then pantry.
- If the outage will be long: **can, dry, or cook and share** freezer meat before it spoils.

## Drying / Dehydrating
- Removes water so bacteria can't grow. Store dried food airtight.
- **Solar dryer:** screened trays in a box with a clear top and vents. Works on sunny, low-humidity days.
- **Oven / wood stove:** lowest heat, door cracked.
- **Fruits:** slice ¼ in, pre-treat with lemon water to prevent browning. Dry until leathery.
- **Vegetables:** blanch first (boil 2–5 min). Dry until brittle.
- **Jerky:** lean meat, sliced thin, marinated with salt. USDA recommends heating meat to **160°F** (poultry 165°F) before or after drying to kill pathogens.
- Herbs: hang bundles in a dry, shaded, airy place.

## Salting & Curing
- Salt draws out water. Historic method for pork, fish and beef.
- **Dry cure:** pack meat in salt (with sugar, optional), turn regularly, keep cool (36–40°F). Takes days to weeks depending on thickness.
- **Brine:** 1 lb salt per gallon of water (strong brine) for corned meats, pickles.
- Commercial curing salts (Prague Powder #1 / #2) prevent botulism in cured sausages and hams. Use **exactly** as directed.
- Salted fish: split, pack in salt, dry. Soak before cooking.

## Fermentation
Lactic-acid fermentation preserves vegetables **and** adds vitamins.
- **Sauerkraut:** shredded cabbage + **2–2.5% salt by weight** (≈ 1½–2 tsp per lb), pound until juicy, pack under its brine, weight it down. 60–70°F for 2–6 weeks. Keep everything **under the brine**.
- **Brined pickles / vegetables:** 3.5–5% salt brine (2–3 Tbsp salt per quart water).
- Kimchi, fermented hot sauce, and sourdough are all the same idea.
- Surface white film (kahm yeast) can be skimmed. **Fuzzy colored mold, slimy texture, or rotten smell: discard.**

## Smoking
- **Cold smoking** (< 90°F) flavors and dries but doesn't cook. Use it only on cured/salted foods.
- **Hot smoking** (165–185°F+) cooks and preserves for a short time.
- Hardwoods: hickory, oak, apple, cherry, maple. **Never** use pine/softwood or treated lumber.

## Root Cellaring
Most root vegetables keep **months** at **32–40°F, 90–95% humidity**:
| Crop | Conditions | Keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 38–40°F, humid, **dark** | 5–8 months |
| Carrots, beets, turnips | 32–35°F, very humid (in damp sand/sawdust) | 4–6 months |
| Cabbage | 32°F, humid (strong odor, keep separate) | 3–4 months |
| Apples | 32°F, humid (keep away from potatoes, as ethylene sprouts them) | 2–6 months |
| **Winter squash, pumpkins** | **50–55°F, dry** | 3–6 months |
| **Sweet potatoes** | **55–60°F**, moderate humidity | 4–6 months |
| Onions, garlic | 32–35°F, **dry**, airy | 6–8 months |

No cellar? A buried trash can, an unheated basement corner, or a straw-insulated pit works.

## Other Methods
- **Lard / confit:** cooked meat sealed under rendered fat, kept cool.
- **Water glassing eggs:** fresh, unwashed eggs submerged in pickling-lime solution (1 oz lime per quart water) last 8–12 months.
- **Sugar:** jams, fruit in heavy syrup, honey.
- **Pemmican:** dried lean meat pounded with rendered fat (roughly 1:1 by weight). Keeps months to years if fat is pure and kept cool.
