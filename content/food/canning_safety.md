# Home Canning Safety & Processing Times

*Based on the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning (2015). Download it for your [Library](/library) — this page is a field summary, not a replacement.*

## The One Rule That Matters
**Low-acid foods (pH > 4.6) MUST be pressure canned.** That means **all vegetables, meats, poultry, fish, beans, soups**.
*Clostridium botulinum* spores survive boiling water (212°F). Only the **240°F** reached in a pressure canner destroys them. Botulism toxin is odorless, often invisible, and can kill.

| Method | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Boiling water bath** | Fruits, jams/jellies, pickles, sauerkraut, salsa (tested recipes), **tomatoes with added acid** |
| **Pressure canner** | Vegetables, meats, poultry, fish, dry beans, stocks, soups, mixed recipes |

Never "oven can," "open-kettle can" (no processing), or invent your own low-acid recipes.

## Pressure Canner Settings by Altitude

| Altitude (ft) | **Weighted gauge** | Dial gauge |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1,000 | **10 lb** | 11 lb |
| 1,001–2,000 | 15 lb | 11 lb |
| 2,001–4,000 | 15 lb | 12 lb |
| 4,001–6,000 | 15 lb | 13 lb |
| 6,001–8,000 | 15 lb | 14 lb |

*Most of Kentucky/Ohio is under 1,000 ft: **10 lb weighted gauge**. Have dial gauges tested yearly.*

**Tomatoes must be acidified:** **1 Tbsp bottled lemon juice** (or ¼ tsp citric acid) per **pint**, **2 Tbsp** (or ½ tsp citric acid) per **quart**.
**Altitude (water bath):** add 5 min at 1,001–3,000 ft; 10 min at 3,001–6,000 ft; 15 min at 6,001–8,000 ft.

## Headspace

| Food | Headspace |
|---|---|
| Jams, jellies | ¼ in |
| Fruits, tomatoes, pickles | ½ in |
| Vegetables, meats, beans (pressure) | 1 in (1¼ in for beans/corn in some recipes, so follow the recipe) |

## Step by Step (Pressure Canner)
1. Inspect jars (no chips), new flat lids, rings.
2. Pack hot jars, leave correct headspace, remove air bubbles, **wipe rims**.
3. Lids on, rings **fingertip tight**.
4. 2–3 in of water in canner, jars on rack.
5. Lock lid, heat, **vent steam for 10 full minutes** before adding the weight/closing petcock.
6. Start timing when the correct pressure is reached. **Keep it at or above pressure the whole time.** If it drops, bring back up and **restart the time from zero**.
7. Turn off heat, let pressure return to **zero naturally**. Never force-cool.
8. Wait 10 min, open lid away from you, let jars sit 10 min more, remove, cool 12–24 hrs undisturbed.
9. Check seals (lid doesn't flex), remove rings, label, store.

## Signs of Spoilage — Do Not Taste
- Bulging or unsealed lid, leaking, spurting liquid on opening
- Cloudy liquid, mold, unnatural smell or color, mushy food
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** Detoxify suspect jars per USDA (boil 30 min) before disposal and don't let pets or children get to them.

## Off-Grid Canning
- A pressure canner works on a wood stove, propane burner, or rocket stove. You just have to manage heat to hold pressure steady.
- Keep **spare gaskets, overpressure plugs, and lids**. Lids are single-use. Reusable lids (Tattler-type) are an option.
