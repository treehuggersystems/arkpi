# Freezing Food

Freezing keeps the most fresh flavor and nutrition with the least work, **as long as the power holds**. In a long grid-down event, freezer food must be eaten, canned, or dried within days. Plan for it. See [Preserving Without Power](/food/preserving_without_power).

## Rules
- Freezer at **0°F (−18°C) or colder**. Use a thermometer.
- Food kept constantly at 0°F stays **safe indefinitely**. The times below are for **quality**.
- Freeze in **small, flat packages** so they freeze fast (better texture) and thaw fast.
- Don't overload: add no more than 2–3 lb per cubic foot of freezer space per day.
- **Remove air:** vacuum sealer, or the water-displacement trick for zip bags (lower the open bag into water, seal at the surface).
- Label everything with contents and date.

## Blanching Vegetables
Blanching (brief boiling, then ice water) stops the enzymes that ruin flavor, color and texture in the freezer. **Don't skip it.**
1. Boil 1 gallon of water per pound of vegetables.
2. Add vegetables; start timing when water **returns to a boil**.
3. Plunge into **ice water for the same time**. Drain well, pack, freeze.

| Vegetable | Blanch (boiling water) |
|---|---|
| Asparagus | small 2 min · medium 3 · large 4 |
| Beans: green, snap, wax | 3 min |
| Beans: lima | small 2 · medium 3 · large 4 |
| Broccoli (1½ in florets) | 3 min |
| Brussels sprouts | small 3 · medium 4 · large 5 |
| Cabbage | wedges 3 · shredded 1½ |
| Carrots | small whole 5 · sliced/diced 2 |
| Cauliflower (1 in florets) | 3 min |
| Corn on the cob | small 7 · medium 9 · large 11 |
| Corn, whole kernel | blanch ears 4 min, then cut |
| Greens (collards) | 3 min |
| Greens (spinach, chard, kale, others) | 2 min |
| Okra | small 3 · large 4 |
| Peas, green | 1½ min |
| Peppers | no blanching needed |
| Summer squash, zucchini (½ in slices) | 3 min |
| Turnips, parsnips (cubes) | 2 min |
| Sweet potatoes | cook until almost tender, then pack |
| Onions | no blanching needed (chop and freeze) |

## Fruit
- **Dry pack:** berries, cherries, grapes. Freeze on a tray, then bag ("IQF").
- **Sugar pack:** sliced peaches, strawberries. Toss with sugar until juicy.
- **Syrup pack:** 30–40% syrup for fruit to be served uncooked.
- Treat light fruit with ascorbic acid to prevent browning.
- Bananas: peel, freeze whole or sliced for baking and smoothies.

## How Long Frozen Food Keeps (quality, at 0°F)
| Food | Months |
|---|---|
| Beef, pork, lamb, venison: steaks, chops, roasts | 4–12 |
| Ground meat | 3–4 |
| Bacon, sausage | 1–2 |
| Chicken / turkey, whole | 12 |
| Chicken / turkey, pieces | 9 |
| Fish, lean (cod, bass, crappie) | 6–8 |
| Fish, fatty (salmon, trout, catfish) | 2–3 |
| Cooked meat, casseroles, soups & stews | 2–3 |
| Blanched vegetables | 8–12 |
| Fruit | 8–12 |
| Bread, baked goods | 2–3 |
| Butter | 6–9 |
| Hard cheese (texture becomes crumbly) | 6 |
| Milk | 3 |
| Eggs: beaten (not in shell) | 12 |

## Freezer Burn
Dry, grayish-brown spots from air exposure. **Safe to eat**, just poor quality. Cut away the spots. Prevent with airtight, air-free packaging.

## Thawing Safely
- In the **refrigerator** (best), in cold water changed every 30 min, or in the microwave and cook immediately.
- **Never** thaw meat on the counter.
- Food thawed in the fridge can be refrozen.

## Power Outage
| Freezer | Stays frozen (door closed) |
|---|---|
| Full | ~48 hours |
| Half-full | ~24 hours |

- Keep it **closed**. Cover with blankets for insulation (don't block vents if it's running).
- **Freezer thermometer** tells you the truth. Food with ice crystals or at **40°F or below** can be refrozen or cooked.
- Long outage? Fill empty space with jugs of water now (they become ice blocks), and have a plan: [pressure can](/food/canning_meat_poultry_fish) the meat, run a generator in blocks, or dry and smoke.
- Winter: snow and outdoor coolers can help, but watch out for animals and thaw/refreeze cycles.
