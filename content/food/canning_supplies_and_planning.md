# Canning Supplies & Planning a Year's Worth

## Equipment
| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Pressure canner** | Stovetop canner that holds **at least 4 quart jars** (e.g. Presto 23-qt, All American). Required for vegetables, meat, beans, soup |
| Water-bath canner | Any deep pot with a rack and lid; jars must be covered by 1–2 in of water |
| Jars | Mason-type canning jars: half-pint, pint, quart, wide- and regular-mouth |
| **Flat lids** | **Single-use.** Reusing them causes seal failures |
| Rings (bands) | Reusable until rusty or bent |
| Jar lifter | Essential for safety |
| Wide-mouth funnel | Keeps rims clean |
| Headspace / bubble tool | Plastic or wood, not metal (can chip jars) |
| Lid wand (magnet) | Lifts lids from hot water |
| Large stockpots, ladle, colander, cutting boards, sharp knives | |
| Kitchen scale, timer, thermometer | |
| Clean towels | Set hot jars on towels, not cold counters |

**Electric multi-cookers (Instant Pot-style) are not tested for safe pressure canning.** Use a stovetop pressure canner. On a wood stove, propane burner, or turkey-fryer base, it works fine off-grid if you can hold pressure steady.

## Stockpile for Grid-Down
- **Flat lids: buy several years' worth.** They're the one consumable you can't improvise. Store cool and dry.
- **Reusable lids** (Tattler / Harvest Guard-style: plastic lid + rubber ring) as a backup; follow their specific instructions.
- **Spare canner gasket, overpressure plug, and weight** for your canner model.
- Bottled lemon juice / citric acid, 5% vinegar (by the gallon), canning salt, pectin (stores ~1 year; long-boil jams don't need it), sugar, Pickle Crisp.
- Extra jars. Thrift stores and yard sales are good sources; inspect rims for chips.

## Planning: How Many Jars?
**Jars per year = servings per week × 52 ÷ servings per jar.**

| Example (family of 4) | Calculation | Jars |
|---|---|---|
| Green beans, side dish twice a week | 2 × 52 = 104 meals, 1 quart feeds 4 | **104 quarts** |
| Tomatoes (sauces, chili, soup) once a week, 1 quart each | 52 | **52 quarts** |
| Applesauce, 3× a week, pint each | 156 | **156 pints** |
| Chicken, once a week, 1 quart | 52 | **52 quarts** |
| Jam, 1 half-pint every 2 weeks | 26 | **26 half-pints** |

A full canner load is **7 quarts or 9 pints** (most pressure canners; check yours). So 104 quarts of green beans = 15 canner loads.

## How Much Fresh Produce?
| Food | Per quart (approx.) | Bushel yields |
|---|---|---|
| Green beans | 1½–2½ lb | 30 lb bushel → 12–20 qt |
| Tomatoes | 2½–3½ lb | 53 lb bushel → 15–21 qt |
| Corn (in husk) | 3–6 lb | 35 lb bushel → 6–11 qt |
| Peaches | 2–3 lb | 48 lb bushel → 16–24 qt |
| Apples (sauce) | 2½–3½ lb | 48 lb bushel → 16–19 qt |
| Boneless meat | 2–2½ lb | — |

## Plan the Season
- **Spring:** asparagus, greens, strawberry jam, rhubarb
- **Early summer:** peas, cherries, berries, dill pickles, green beans
- **Mid-late summer:** tomatoes, corn, peaches, peppers, salsa, more beans
- **Fall:** apples (sauce, butter, slices), pears, winter squash cubes, sweet potatoes, venison
- **Winter:** dried beans, stocks, soups, meat from the freezer: canning in winter warms the house
