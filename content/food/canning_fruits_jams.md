# Canning Fruits, Tomatoes, Jams & Jellies

Most fruits are naturally **high-acid**, so a **boiling water bath** is enough. **Tomatoes are borderline** and must have acid added. Use the [Canning Lookup](/tools/canning) to adjust for altitude.

### ⚠ Always acidify tomatoes
Modern tomato varieties can be too low in acid to be safe. Add **to each jar**:

| Jar | Bottled lemon juice | or citric acid |
|---|---|---|
| Pint | 1 Tbsp | ¼ tsp |
| Quart | 2 Tbsp | ½ tsp |

Use **bottled** lemon juice (consistent acidity), not fresh. Add a little sugar if the acid taste bothers you. **Don't** use overripe or damaged tomatoes, or tomatoes from dead/frost-killed vines.

## Syrups for Canning Fruit
Sugar helps color, flavor and texture. It's **not** needed for safety, so you can also pack fruit in water, apple juice, or white grape juice.

| Syrup | Sugar | Water | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extra light | 1¼ cups | 5½ cups | Sweet fruit, low sugar |
| Light | 2¼ cups | 5¼ cups | Apples, sweet cherries, grapes, pears |
| Medium | 3¼ cups | 5 cups | Apricots, peaches, sour cherries |
| Heavy | 4¼ cups | 4¼ cups | Very sour fruit |

*Each makes about 6½–7 cups, enough for a canner load of about 9 pints. Heat until sugar dissolves.*

## Prevent Browning
Light-colored fruit (apples, pears, peaches, apricots) darkens when cut. Drop slices into **ascorbic acid solution** (1 tsp pure ascorbic acid or six 500 mg vitamin C tablets, crushed, per gallon of water) or a commercial fruit-fresh product while you work.

## Peeling Peaches & Tomatoes
Dip in boiling water **30–60 seconds**, then into ice water. Skins slip right off.

## Jams & Jellies
| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Jelly** | Clear, from fruit juice |
| **Jam** | Crushed or chopped fruit |
| **Preserves** | Whole or large pieces in syrup/jelly |
| **Butter** | Fruit pulp cooked down until thick (apple butter) |
| **Marmalade** | Soft jelly with citrus peel |

### Gel needs four things in balance
**Fruit, pectin, acid, sugar.** Don't cut the sugar in a regular-pectin recipe or it won't set. Use **low/no-sugar pectin** for reduced-sugar jams.

### Long-boil (no added pectin) method
Works for high-pectin fruit (tart apples, crabapples, blackberries, plums, grapes, cranberries).
- Cook fruit and sugar to the **gel point: 8°F above water's boiling point (220°F at sea level)**.
- **Cold plate test:** put a plate in the freezer. Drop a little jam on it, return 1 minute, push with a finger. If it wrinkles, it's done.
- **Sheet test:** jam slides off a cold metal spoon in a single sheet rather than drops.

### Processing jam
Ladle hot into hot jars, ¼ in headspace, wipe rims, lids, process **5 min** in sterilized jars (boil empty jars 10 min first) or **10 min** if jars weren't sterilized. Add altitude time.

Freezer jam (uncooked, special pectin) skips processing entirely: store in the freezer.

## Juices
- **Grape juice:** crush, heat to 190°F (don't boil), strain through cloth, let sediment settle 24–48 hrs in the fridge, pour off, reheat, pack, process 5 min.
- **Apple juice / cider:** heat to 190°F, process 5 min (pints/quarts).
