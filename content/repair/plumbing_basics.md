# Plumbing Basics & Emergency Fixes

## Know Your Shutoffs (find them TODAY)
- **Main water shutoff**: where the line enters the house (basement wall, crawlspace, utility closet) and/or at the meter by the street (needs a meter key).
- **Fixture shutoffs** under sinks and behind toilets (turn clockwise).
- **Water heater**: cold inlet valve on top. Also know the gas valve or breaker.
- Exercise valves once a year. Old ones seize.

## Stopping a Leak
| Situation | Quick fix |
|---|---|
| Pinhole in copper / pipe | Shut water, dry, wrap with **self-fusing silicone tape** or apply **epoxy putty**; or a **pipe repair clamp** (rubber pad + band clamp) |
| Burst or cracked section | Cut out the bad section, join with a **push-to-connect fitting** (SharkBite) or compression coupling. Works on copper, PEX, CPVC |
| Leaking compression fitting | Tighten ¼ turn with two wrenches (one to hold) |
| Dripping faucet | Replace cartridge/washer (take old one to match) |
| Leaking drain trap | Hand-tighten slip nuts; replace washer |

**Tool kit:** adjustable wrenches ×2, pipe wrench, tubing cutter, plumber's tape (PTFE), epoxy putty, push-fit couplings in your pipe sizes (½", ¾"), repair clamps, plunger, closet auger, bucket.

## Running Toilet
1. Lift the tank lid. Water running into the overflow tube? Lower the **float** (adjust screw or clip).
2. Water slowly leaking into bowl? Replace the **flapper** (cheap, universal). Test: a few drops of food coloring in tank; color in bowl after 15 min = bad flapper.
3. Fill valve hissing / won't shut: replace fill valve.

## Flushing Without Running Water
Pour **1–2 gallons of water quickly** directly into the bowl. It flushes by gravity. Use greywater (washing water) for this. If the sewer is backing up or the municipal system is down, use a [bucket toilet](/homestead/sanitation_hygiene) instead.

## Frozen Pipes
- **Signs:** no water from a faucet, frost on exposed pipe.
- Open the faucet (so melting water/steam can escape), then warm the pipe **from the faucet end back** with a hair dryer, heat lamp, warm towels, or heat tape.
- **Never use an open flame.** It starts fires and can burst pipes.
- Prevention: let faucets on exterior walls **drip**, open cabinet doors, insulate pipes, seal drafts.

## Winterizing a House With No Heat
If the house will freeze for days:
1. **Shut the main water valve.**
2. Turn off and drain the **water heater** (turn off power/gas first).
3. Open **all faucets** (hot and cold, highest floor first), then drain from the **lowest** point/faucet.
4. **Flush toilets** and sponge/bail out remaining tank and bowl water.
5. Pour **RV/plumbing antifreeze (non-toxic, propylene glycol)** into every **drain trap**, toilet bowl and tank. **Not** automotive antifreeze.
6. Blow out lines with an air compressor at low pressure (30–40 psi) if possible.
7. Disconnect garden hoses; drain outdoor spigots.

## Well Systems
- No power = no well pump. A **hand pump** (installed alongside the submersible) or a generator/solar inverter sized for the pump surge are the long-term answers.
- Pressure tank holds a few gallons of usable water.
- Shock-chlorinate a well after flooding before drinking.

## Sewer / Septic
- Septic systems work without power (unless they have a lift pump) but **don't overload** them. Space out laundry and water use.
- Municipal sewer may back up if lift stations lose power. Plug floor drains or use backflow valves; switch to bucket toilets.
