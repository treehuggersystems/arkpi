# Sharpening Knives, Axes & Chainsaws

A sharp tool is a **safe** tool — dull edges slip and need force.

## Knives (Whetstone)
| Use | Angle per side |
|---|---|
| Kitchen / slicing | 15–17° |
| Pocket / general | 17–20° |
| Hard-use outdoor / chopping | 20–25° |

**Find the angle:** 90° vertical → halve to 45° → halve to ~22° → a bit lower ≈ 17–20°. Or stack 2–3 coins under the spine.

### Process
1. Soak water stones 10 min (oil stones get a few drops of honing oil; diamond plates: water or dry).
2. **Coarse (~400 grit)**: hold the angle, push edge-first across the stone in sweeping strokes heel to tip. Do one side until you feel a **burr** (a tiny lip) along the whole opposite edge.
3. Flip, repeat on the other side.
4. **Fine (~1000–6000 grit)**: alternate sides with lighter strokes to refine.
5. **Strop** on leather or cardboard (edge-trailing) to remove the burr.
6. Test: slices paper cleanly, or catches on a thumbnail.

**Improvised stones:** the unglazed ring on the bottom of a ceramic mug, a smooth river stone (fine sandstone), a car window edge.

### Honing Rod
A steel rod **realigns** an edge between sharpenings: 5–6 light alternating strokes at the edge angle.

## Axes, Hatchets, Machetes
- Use a **mill bastard file** (10") then a puck stone.
- File **into** the edge (away from you), with the axe secured. **Wear gloves.** File strokes cut on the push only.
- Axe edge: convex profile, roughly **25–30°** per side. Maintain the original shape; keep the bit's curve.
- Machete: ~25–30°, file then stone.
- Check for loose heads: soak wooden handles' heads overnight in water to swell temporarily; long-term, re-wedge.

## Chainsaw Chains
| Chain pitch | Round file size |
|---|---|
| ¼" | 5/32" (4.0 mm) |
| 3/8" low profile | **5/32" (4.0 mm)** |
| .325" | **3/16" (4.8 mm)** |
| 3/8" standard | **7/32" (5.5 mm)** |
| .404" | 7/32" (5.5 mm) |

*Pitch is stamped on the drive links or bar. Always confirm with your saw's manual.*

### Process
1. Saw off, chain brake on, bar in a vise or on a stump. Gloves.
2. Use a **file guide**. Typical top-plate angle **30°**, file held level (or 10° down per chain spec).
3. File **from the inside of the cutter outward**, 3–5 even strokes per tooth. Do all cutters on one side, then turn the saw around.
4. Mark your starting tooth with a marker.
5. Every 3–5 sharpenings, check **depth gauges (rakers)** with a depth gauge tool and flat file. Typically 0.025" (0.65 mm) below the cutter.
6. **Signs you need to sharpen:** saw makes **dust instead of chips**, pulls to one side, needs pressure to cut, smokes.
7. Hit dirt or a rock? Sharpen immediately.

## Other Tools
- **Shovels / hoes:** file a 45° bevel on the working edge.
- **Scissors:** sharpen only the bevel side; never the flat inside faces.
- **Mower blades:** file or grind to the original angle (~30°), **balance** the blade on a nail before reinstalling.
- Keep tools oiled to prevent rust (any oil; wipe on, wipe off).
