# Small Engine Troubleshooting

Every gasoline engine needs **fuel, air, spark, and compression**. Find which one is missing.

## No-Start Checklist (in order)

### 1. Basics (most problems end here)
- [ ] **Fuel valve ON**, gas in the tank, kill switch / ignition **ON**
- [ ] **Choke** closed for a cold start, open once it fires
- [ ] Oil level OK (many engines have **low-oil shutdown** sensors)
- [ ] Generator: all loads disconnected for starting

### 2. Fuel
- **Old gas** is the #1 cause. Gas older than ~3 months (unstabilized) varnishes and loses volatility. Drain and refill with fresh.
- Remove the air filter, spray a **one-second shot of starting fluid** (or a few drops of fresh gas) into the intake, then pull.
  - **Fires briefly then dies → fuel delivery problem** (carburetor, fuel line, filter, tank vent).
  - **Nothing → spark** (or severe compression) problem.
- Check fuel filter, fuel line for cracks/kinks, tank cap vent (loosen cap: if it runs, the vent is clogged).

### 3. Spark
- Pull the plug: **wet with gas** = flooded or no spark. **Dry** = no fuel. **Black, sooty** = rich/choke stuck. **White, blistered** = lean/overheating.
- Test: reconnect the plug to the wire, hold the threads against bare metal on the engine (insulated pliers), pull the cord. Look for a **strong blue spark**.
- No spark → check kill-switch wire (disconnect it and retest), plug (replace), ignition coil air gap (typically ~0.010" — use a business card) and coil.
- **Gap the plug** per manual (often 0.028–0.030").

### 4. Flooded
- Choke **open**, throttle wide open, pull 5–10 times. Or remove the plug, pull to clear, dry the plug, reinstall.

### 5. Air
- Clogged air filter → runs rich, black smoke, low power. Clean foam filters in soapy water (oil lightly); replace paper.

### 6. Compression
- Pull cord feels too **easy** with no resistance? Possible low compression (worn rings, valve problem, or blown head gasket). A compression gauge reading below ~90 psi on small engines is low. Usually needs deeper repair.

## Cleaning a Carburetor (the usual fix for old gas)
1. Photograph linkages first. Shut off fuel, drain the bowl (screw at the bottom).
2. Remove the **float bowl**. Look for varnish, white crust, water.
3. Remove the **main jet** and **emulsion tube**. Clean all holes with carb cleaner and a fine wire (strand of wire brush, guitar string). **Hold up to light to confirm holes are clear.** Never enlarge them.
4. Clean the **pilot (idle) jet**. It's tiny and usually the culprit for no-idle/surging.
5. Check the float and needle valve for free movement and a clean seat.
6. Reassemble with the bowl gasket seated. Turn fuel on and check for leaks.

**Surging / "hunting" at idle** = partially clogged pilot jet or air leak.

## Running Problems
| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Starts then dies | Clogged pilot jet, choke stuck, tank vent clogged, low oil |
| Runs only on choke | Lean: dirty carb, air leak at intake gasket |
| Black smoke | Rich: dirty air filter, choke stuck closed, float stuck |
| Blue/white smoke | Burning oil: overfilled, tipped over, worn rings |
| Loses power under load | Dirty air filter, fuel restriction, dull chain (saws) |
| Overheating | Blocked cooling fins (clean debris), low oil, lean mixture |

## 2-Stroke Engines (chainsaws, trimmers)
- Run on **gas + 2-stroke oil**. Common ratios **50:1** (2.6 oz oil per gallon) or **40:1** (3.2 oz/gal). Check your manual. **Never** run straight gas: it seizes the engine in minutes.
- Mixed fuel goes bad faster: mix only what you'll use in ~30 days unless stabilized.

## Storage
- Stabilize fuel and run 5 min to get it into the carb, **or** run the carb dry.
- Change oil, clean the air filter, fog the cylinder (spray fogging oil in the plug hole) for long storage.
