# Vehicle Emergency Repairs

## Jump-Starting
1. Both cars off, in park/neutral, parking brakes on. Don't let the cars touch.
2. **RED** clamp to the **dead battery +**
3. Other **RED** clamp to the **good battery +**
4. **BLACK** clamp to the **good battery −**
5. Last **BLACK** clamp to **unpainted metal on the dead car's engine block or a ground point**, away from the battery (batteries vent hydrogen).
6. Start the good car, wait 2–5 min, then start the dead car.
7. Remove clamps in **reverse order**.
- Won't crank at all, just clicks: clean corroded terminals (baking soda + water, wire brush).
- **Lithium jump packs** are compact and hold charge for months. Keep one in each vehicle.

## Changing a Flat Tire
1. Hazards on, flat level ground, parking brake, chock the opposite wheel.
2. **Loosen lug nuts ½ turn while the tire is still on the ground.**
3. Jack at the **designated lift point** (see manual, usually reinforced pinch weld behind front wheel / in front of rear wheel).
4. Remove nuts, swap tires, hand-tighten nuts.
5. Lower until tire touches, tighten in a **star pattern**, lower fully, final tighten (typical cars 76–100 ft-lb).
6. Check spare pressure; donut spares: max ~50 mph, short distances.

## Plugging a Tire (punctures in the tread only)
1. Find the hole (soapy water bubbles). Pull out the nail/screw.
2. Ream the hole with the **reamer tool** from the kit.
3. Thread a sticky plug halfway through the insertion tool, push it in until ~½ in remains outside, pull the tool straight out.
4. Trim, inflate, check for leaks. **Sidewall damage can't be plugged.**

## Overheating
- Temp gauge climbing: **turn off AC, turn heater on full hot + fan high**. It dumps engine heat.
- Pull over, shut off, **never open a hot radiator cap**. Pressurized coolant causes severe burns. Wait 30+ minutes.
- Check coolant level in the overflow tank, look for leaks, a broken belt, or a non-spinning electric fan.
- Emergency: plain **water** can top up coolant (drain and replace with proper mix later, and before freezing weather).

## Belts & Hoses
- Squealing on startup = loose/glazed belt. Broken serpentine belt = no alternator, no water pump on many cars, so **stop soon**.
- Carry a spare serpentine belt (and know the routing diagram, often on a sticker under the hood).
- Small hose leak: **self-fusing silicone tape** or a hose repair kit. Release pressure first.

## Electrical
- Something stopped working? Check the **fuse box** (manual shows layout). Carry spare fuses of each rating.
- Dim lights / battery light on = alternator problem. A healthy charging system reads **13.8–14.7 V** at the battery with the engine running. Under 12.6 = not charging.

## Fluids Quick Reference
| Fluid | Check | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Engine oil | Dipstick, engine off & level | Between the marks. Stock a few quarts |
| Coolant | Overflow tank (cold) | 50/50 antifreeze/water |
| Brake fluid | Reservoir | Low = worn pads or leak. **Don't drive if pedal is soft** |
| Transmission | Dipstick (some running/hot) | Pink/red, not burnt-smelling |
| Power steering | Reservoir | Whining when turning = low |

## Keeping Vehicles Running Long-Term
- Stock: oil + filters, air filters, wiper blades, belts, fuses, bulbs, brake pads, tire plug kit, 12 V compressor, basic socket set.
- **Older (pre-1980s), carbureted, non-computer vehicles** are simplest to repair without diagnostic tools and are least EMP-sensitive.
- Diesel stores longer than gasoline with treatment.
- A **bicycle** is the ultimate grid-down vehicle. See [Bicycle Repair](/repair/bicycle_repair).
