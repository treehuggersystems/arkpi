# Emergency Frequencies Quick Card

> **In a real emergency, FCC rules (47 CFR 97.403 / 95.1749) allow any station to use any means of radio communication to protect life and property when normal communications are unavailable.** Outside of that, transmit only where you're licensed.

## Listen First (Receive-Only, No License)

| Service | Frequency | What you'll hear |
|---|---|---|
| NOAA Weather Radio | 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525, 162.550 MHz | Weather + civil emergency alerts (EAS) |
| AM broadcast | 530–1700 kHz | News; strongest stations carry for hundreds of miles at night |
| Aviation emergency | 121.500 MHz (AM) | Aircraft distress / ELT beacons |
| WWV / WWVH time | 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 MHz | Exact time + space-weather & storm reports |
| CHU (Canada) time | 3.330, 7.850, 14.670 MHz | Exact time |
| Shortwave broadcast | 5.9–15.8 MHz bands | International news when domestic stations are down |

## License-Free Two-Way

| Service | Channel / frequency | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **FRS / GMRS** | **Ch 1** — 462.5625 MHz | Common default / calling |
| FRS / GMRS | **Ch 20** — 462.675 MHz | Traditional emergency & traveler assistance (REACT) |
| FRS / GMRS | Ch 3 — 462.6125 MHz | "3-3-3" community check-in (see below) |
| **CB** | **Ch 9** — 27.065 MHz | Official emergency channel |
| CB | **Ch 19** — 27.185 MHz | Highway / truckers — road & traffic info |
| MURS | 151.820, 151.880, 151.940, 154.570, 154.600 MHz | 2 W license-free VHF — better range than FRS outdoors |
| **Marine VHF** | **Ch 16** — 156.800 MHz | International distress, safety & calling (on the water) |
| Marine VHF | Ch 9 — 156.450 MHz | Secondary calling (boaters) |

*GMRS on Ch 15–22 at higher power and repeaters requires a GMRS license ($35, 10 years, no exam, covers your family).*

## Amateur (Ham) Radio — License Required Except in Emergencies

| Band | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **2 m** | **146.520 MHz FM** | National simplex calling — the #1 ham frequency to monitor |
| 70 cm | 446.000 MHz FM | National simplex calling (UHF) |
| 1.25 m | 223.500 MHz FM | Simplex calling |
| 6 m | 52.525 MHz FM | Simplex calling; occasional long-distance "sporadic-E" |
| 10 m | 29.600 MHz FM | FM calling; long range when the sun cooperates |
| 20 m | 14.300 MHz USB | Maritime Mobile / InterContinental emergency nets |
| 40 m | 7.150–7.300 MHz LSB | Regional nets, daytime 300–1,000 mi; night longer |
| 80 m | 3.800–4.000 MHz LSB | Regional nets, evening/night; best NVIS (0–300 mi) |
| 60 m | 5330.5, 5346.5, 5357.0, 5371.5, 5403.5 kHz USB | Channelized; used for federal/ham interoperability |

## The "3-3-3" Community Plan

A widely shared grassroots convention for finding each other after a disaster:

- **Channel 3** — FRS/GMRS ch 3 or CB ch 3
- **At 3 o'clock** (3 AM / 3 PM), and then every 3 hours
- **For 3 minutes** of listening, then brief calls

It's informal — agree on your own times and channels in your [PACE plan](/comms/pace_comms_plan).

## Scan Priorities After an Event

1. **NOAA weather / AM broadcast** — what happened and how big is it?
2. **146.520 MHz and local repeaters** — local ham traffic and ARES nets
3. **CB 9 / 19 and FRS 1 / 20** — neighbors and travelers
4. **HF 40 m / 80 m evenings** — regional picture beyond your horizon

## Priority
- **Write your local repeater frequencies + tones down on paper.** Keep it with the radio.
