# PACE Communications Plan

When cell networks go down, a plan written *before* the event is what reunites people. **PACE** gives you four layers so one failure doesn't leave you blind.

## The Four Layers

| Layer | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **P**rimary | Cell phone / text | Texts often get through when calls fail |
| **A**lternate | GMRS radio, ch 17 (tone 141.3) | Pre-agreed channel + privacy tone |
| **C**ontingency | Ham 2 m simplex 146.550 / local repeater | Needs licensed operator |
| **E**mergency | Physical rally point + written note | Works when *all* electronics fail |

## Check-In Schedule (Radio Windows)

Battery life matters. Instead of leaving radios on all day:
- Agree on **windows**, e.g. *:00–:05 every 2 hours from 0600–2200*.
- Scan the alternate channel first, then contingency.
- If you miss two windows, go to **rally point #1**. If no one by the deadline, **rally point #2**.

## Rally Points
- **#1:** Walking distance, obvious, sheltered — a neighbor's porch, a church.
- **#2:** Outside the neighborhood — in case of fire, flooding or evacuation.
- **Long-term:** Relatives' home or a property out of the area, with routes planned.
- Mark them on the [map](/map) as waypoints and print the coordinates.

## Out-of-Area Contact
Long-distance calls often connect when local calls don't. Choose someone **far away** that every family member texts "I'm OK, at ___" — they relay.

## Leaving Notes
Standard note when you leave a location: **who, when (date/time), where you went, route, condition**.
Example: *"Sam & kids — Sat 14:30 — went to Rally #2 via Main St — all OK."*

## Practice
- Do a **radio drill** quarterly: everyone checks in from their usual daytime location.
- Walk the route to each rally point with the kids.
- Keep spare batteries with radios and the plan in a zip bag.

## Priority
- **The rally point is the plan that never needs batteries.**
