# Animal Tracking & Sign

Tracking tells you **what's around, where it goes, and when**. That means where to hunt or trap, where water is, and what's been near your camp or garden.

## Reading a Track: The Basics
- **Count the toes.** 4 toes = dog, cat, rabbit family. 5 toes = raccoon, opossum, bear, skunk, weasels, beaver, people. 2 "toes" (split hoof) = deer, hogs. 3 forward toes = birds.
- **Claws or no claws?** Canines (dogs, coyotes, foxes) usually show claws. Cats (bobcats, house cats) retract them, so no claw marks.
- **Shape:** canine tracks are **oval** (longer than wide); cat tracks are **round**.
- **Measure** length and width, and the distance between tracks (stride). Carry a small tape or know your hand: the width of your four fingers is about 3 in.
- **Look at a series,** not one print. The pattern tells you more than any single track.

## Common Tracks (Eastern US)
| Animal | Track | Size (length) | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **White-tailed deer** | Split heart shape | 2–3½ in | Pointed end = direction of travel. Dewclaws show behind in mud, snow, or when running |
| **Feral hog** | Split hoof, rounder | 2–3 in | Blunter, rounder toes than deer; dewclaws set wider; rooted-up ground nearby |
| **Coyote** | Oval, 4 toes, claws | 2¼–3 in | Neat, compact; outer toes tucked in; an **X** fits between toes and heel pad. Travels in a **straight line** |
| **Domestic dog** | Rounder, 4 toes, claws | varies | Toes splay out; claws prominent; trail **wanders** all over |
| **Red / gray fox** | Small oval, 4 toes | 1¾–2½ in | Red fox: furry feet blur the print, bar across heel pad. Very straight trail |
| **Bobcat** | Round, 4 toes, **no claws** | ~2 in | Heel pad has 3 lobes on its back edge; leading toe offset |
| **Raccoon** | Little hand, 5 long toes | 2–3 in | Often found in **pairs** (a hind beside a front). Near water |
| **Opossum** | 5 toes, star-like | 1½–2 in | Hind foot has an **opposable thumb** pointing sideways |
| **Black bear** | Big, 5 toes | Front 4–5 in wide; hind 6–7 in long | Hind print looks like a wide human foot. Eastern Kentucky especially |
| **Striped skunk** | 5 toes, claws | 1–1½ in | Small, shuffling; long front claws show |
| **Beaver** | Webbed hind foot | Hind 5–7 in | Tail drag; chewed "pencil" stumps; dams and lodges |
| **Cottontail rabbit** | Groups of 4 | Hind 3–3½ in | **Hind feet land ahead** of the front feet; fronts one behind the other |
| **Gray squirrel** | Groups of 4 | Hind ~2 in | Hind feet ahead; **fronts side by side** (rabbit fronts are staggered). Trail goes tree to tree |
| **Wild turkey** | 3 toes forward | 4–5 in | Long middle toe; scratched-up leaves where they fed |
| **Human** | Shoe or bare foot | — | Tread pattern; stride 2–3 ft walking |

## Gaits: How It Moves Tells You What It Is
| Pattern | Who | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagonal walker** | Deer, canines, cats | Neat line of single prints; hind foot lands in the front print ("direct register") |
| **Pacer** | Wide-bodied: raccoon, opossum, bear, beaver, skunk | Left front + left hind move together; prints in pairs, waddling trail |
| **Bounder** | Weasels, mink, otter | Pairs of prints side by side, evenly spaced bounds |
| **Galloper / hopper** | Rabbits, squirrels, mice | Groups of 4, hind feet in front |

When an animal speeds up, its stride lengthens and the pattern changes. A deer's clean line becomes groups of 4 with splayed toes and dewclaws when it bolts.

## Aging a Track
How old is it? Compare with a fresh print you make yourself next to it, in the same soil.
- **Fresh:** crisp, sharp edges; moist soil pushed up; bent grass still springing back; no debris in it.
- **Hours old:** edges starting to crumble or dry; dew or frost **on top** of the track means it was made before the dew/frost formed (that is, before dawn).
- **Older:** rain-pitted, leaves or debris blown in, insects or spider webs across it, edges rounded.
- Know the weather: if it rained at midnight and the tracks have no rain pits, they were made after midnight.

## Sign: Clues Besides Tracks
| Sign | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Trails / runs** | Worn paths through grass or brush, often between bedding and feeding/water. Deer trails, rabbit runs under brush |
| **Beds** | Oval flattened areas in grass or snow (deer beds ~4 ft long). Often on high ground with cover |
| **Rubs** | Bark scraped off saplings at knee-to-waist height: buck rubs in fall |
| **Scrapes** | Pawed bare patch under an overhanging branch: buck breeding sign in fall |
| **Browse** | Deer have no upper front teeth: twigs look **torn/ragged**. Rabbits clip twigs at a clean **45° angle** |
| **Scat** | Deer: pellets. Rabbit: round marbles. Coyote/fox: twisted, tapered, full of hair and berries. Raccoon: blunt, often on logs or at latrines |
| **Feeding** | Gnawed nuts (squirrels: split shells; mice: small round holes), dug-up acorn caches, turkey scratching, hog rooting |
| **Hair & feathers** | On barbed wire, brush, fence crossings |
| **Dens & burrows** | Size of opening, fresh dirt, tracks, smell |

## How to Track Well
- **Track with the light low** (early morning, late afternoon) and look **toward** the sun, keeping the trail between you and the light. Shadows make faint prints pop.
- Don't step on the trail. Walk beside it.
- Lost the trail? Mark the last sure track and circle outward.
- Go **slow**. Look ahead along the likely path, not just at your feet.
- Soft ground, mud, sand, creek banks and fresh snow are where you'll learn fastest.
