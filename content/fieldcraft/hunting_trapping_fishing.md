# Hunting, Trapping & Fishing for Survival

> **Know the law.** Many methods here (snares, trotlines, fish traps, night hunting, hunting out of season) are **illegal** in normal times without the proper license and season. Practice legal methods now. Use the rest only in a true survival emergency.

## Priorities
- **Fish and small game** (squirrels, rabbits, groundhogs) are easier to get than deer and need no long-term preservation.
- **Passive methods** (traps, trotlines) work while you do other things. Set many.
- **Calories matter.** Lean rabbit alone can't sustain you ("rabbit starvation"): you need **fat** from fish, nuts, pork, deer fat, or stored oil alongside lean meat.

## Snares (survival use)
A loop of wire that tightens around an animal moving along a trail.
- **Where:** on clear **runs and trails** (rabbit runs under brush, squirrel paths along logs, fence gaps), not randomly. Look for tracks and droppings.
- **Material:** thin steel snare wire, or picture-hanging wire; paracord inner strands work poorly.
- **Rabbit snare:** loop about **4 in** across, bottom about **3–4 in** off the ground (about a fist), set across a run. Anchor solidly to a stake or sapling. Use sticks to narrow the trail so the animal has to go through.
- **Squirrel pole:** a long pole leaned against a tree squirrels use, with several small loops (2–2½ in) along it.
- **Check snares at least daily.** Leaving animals suffering is wasteful and cruel.
- Handle as little as possible (human scent), and set many: 10+ snares to catch 1–2 animals.

## Deadfalls
A heavy weight (flat rock or log) propped by a trigger that collapses when bait is disturbed.
- **Figure-4 deadfall:** three notched sticks (upright, diagonal lever, horizontal bait stick) in a "4" shape support the weight. Animal tugs bait → trigger collapses → weight falls.
- **Paiute deadfall:** a very sensitive trigger using a short cord and toggle; works well for small animals.
- Weight should be **3–5× the animal's weight**. Bait with nuts, peanut butter, fruit, or meat scraps.
- **Danger:** deadfalls can crush fingers when setting. Set them carefully. Keep children and pets away.

## Field Dressing a Deer
1. Confirm it's dead (no blink when the eye is touched). Tag it if laws apply.
2. Wear gloves. Roll it on its back, head uphill.
3. Make a shallow cut through the hide from the **pelvis to the breastbone**, lifting the skin with two fingers so the knife **doesn't puncture the stomach or intestines**.
4. Cut around the anus to free it (tie it off if you can), and remove the genitals.
5. Cut the diaphragm (the sheet of muscle separating chest and belly), reach up and cut the windpipe as far forward as you can.
6. Roll the deer to its side and pull the entrails out. Save the **heart and liver** (good to eat if the liver looks healthy: no spots or abscesses).
7. Drain blood, prop the cavity open with a stick, and **cool the carcass quickly**: hang it in shade; in warm weather, quarter it and get it on ice or into the canner within hours.
8. Process: [can the meat](/food/canning_meat_poultry_fish), dry it into [jerky](/food/dehydrating_guide), or smoke it.

## Small Game
- **Squirrel / rabbit:** cut a slit across the back, insert fingers, pull the skin apart toward head and feet. Remove the head and feet, open the belly, and remove the guts.
- **Tularemia ("rabbit fever"):** wear gloves when cleaning rabbits. Avoid any rabbit that acts sluggish or sick, or that has white spots on the liver. **Cook all wild game thoroughly** (160°F).
- **Don't eat** animals that were sick, behaving strangely, or found dead.
- In warm months, wild rabbits and squirrels can carry parasites like warbles. Cut them out; the meat is still fine when cooked.

## Fishing Without Store Gear
- **Gorge hook:** a sliver of bone or hardwood pointed at both ends, line tied in the middle, buried lengthwise in bait. The fish swallows it, the line pulls it sideways and it lodges.
- **Improvised hooks:** safety pins, bent wire, thorns (hawthorn), carved bone.
- **Line:** paracord inner strands, thread, dental floss, fishing line from the kit.
- **Bait:** worms (dig in damp soil, under logs), grubs, crickets, grasshoppers, minnows, crayfish, bits of guts from other fish/game.
- **Trotline / setline:** a long line staked across a creek or river with many baited hooks hanging every few feet. Check morning and evening. Catfish are the usual catch in Ohio Valley rivers.
- **Fish trap:** a funnel of sticks or wire (or a cut plastic bottle with the top inverted inside) baited and set in a creek. Fish swim in and can't find the way out.
- **Stone weir:** a V or U of stones in a shallow stream that funnels fish into a pool you can close off.
- **Night fishing** for catfish with stink bait or liver.
- **Noodling, spears, gigging frogs**: check local laws.

## Cleaning Fish
- **Scale** (scrape tail to head with a knife back), or **skin** catfish (cut around behind the head, pull the skin off with pliers).
- Slit the belly from vent to gills, remove the guts, cut off the head.
- **Fillet** larger fish: cut behind the gills to the backbone, turn the blade and run it along the spine to the tail.
- Eat fish fresh, or smoke, salt, or can it (pints, 100 minutes; see [Canning Meat & Fish](/food/canning_meat_poultry_fish)).
- Avoid fish from polluted water (industrial outfalls, below farms with algae blooms). Follow local fish-consumption advisories for mercury.

## Edible Insects & Other Protein
Grasshoppers, crickets, grubs, and earthworms are high in protein. **Cook them** (roast or boil) to kill parasites, and remove wings and legs from grasshoppers. Avoid brightly colored, hairy, or bad-smelling insects. Crayfish (boil until red) are excellent, and are abundant in Kentucky creeks.
