# Weather Lore & Reading the Sky

Before radio and radar, farmers, sailors and frontiersmen read the weather from the sky, the wind and the animals. Much of that lore is real science in plain words. Use it together with the [Wind Chill & Heat tool](/tools/weather) and a cheap barometer.

## Cloud Guide
| Cloud | Looks like | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Cirrus** ("mares' tails") | High, thin, wispy streaks | Fair now; if thickening and increasing, a warm front and rain/snow in 24–36 hrs |
| **Cirrostratus** | Thin milky veil; **halo** around sun or moon | Moisture increasing: rain or snow often within 12–24 hrs |
| **Cirrocumulus / altocumulus** ("mackerel sky") | Rows of small puffs like fish scales | Change coming; unsettled weather within a day or so |
| **Altostratus** | Gray sheet; sun looks like it's behind frosted glass | Rain or snow likely within hours |
| **Nimbostratus** | Low, dark, featureless gray | Steady rain or snow now |
| **Stratus** | Low gray blanket, fog lifted | Drizzle, gloom; usually not severe |
| **Cumulus** (fair weather) | Puffy, flat-bottomed, scattered | Fair, especially if they shrink by evening |
| **Towering cumulus** | Puffs growing tall like cauliflower by late morning | Showers or thunderstorms later in the day |
| **Cumulonimbus** | Huge, anvil-shaped top, dark base | **Thunderstorm**: lightning, downpours, hail, wind, possibly tornadoes |
| **Lenticular** | Smooth lens/saucer shapes near hills | Strong winds aloft |

## Old Sayings: True or False?
| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *"Red sky at night, sailor's delight; red sky at morning, sailors take warning."* | **Mostly true.** Weather moves west to east in the US. Red evening sky = clear air to the west; red morning sky = sun lighting clouds/moisture arriving from the west |
| *"Mackerel sky and mares' tails make tall ships carry low sails."* | **True.** Cirrus and cirrocumulus often precede a front |
| *"Ring around the moon, rain real soon."* | **Often true.** A halo means high cirrostratus ahead of a front |
| *"When dew is on the grass, rain will never come to pass."* | **Usually true.** Heavy dew means a clear, calm night: fair weather |
| *"Rainbow in the morning, shepherds take warning; rainbow at night, shepherd's delight."* | **True.** A morning rainbow is in the west, so the rain is to the west and coming your way |
| *"Smoke that hangs low or curls down means rain; smoke that rises straight means fair."* | **Often true.** Low pressure and moist air keep smoke down |
| *"Swallows fly low before rain."* | **Some truth.** Insects stay low in humid air before storms, and birds follow them |
| *"Leaves show their backs before a storm."* | **Some truth.** Shifting, gusty winds ahead of storms flip leaves |
| *"Sounds carry farther before rain."* | **Some truth.** Low clouds and moist air can carry sound |
| *"Pine cones close when rain is coming."* | **True for humidity.** Cones close in damp air and open in dry air |
| *"Aching joints mean rain."* | Many people swear by it (falling pressure); not reliable |
| *"Woolly bear caterpillars predict winter."* | **False.** Fun, but no predictive value |
| Groundhog Day | **False.** |

## Wind & Pressure
- **Buys Ballot's law:** stand with your **back to the wind**. In the Northern Hemisphere, **low pressure (bad weather) is to your left** and a little ahead.
- **Backing wind** (shifting counter-clockwise, for example south → southeast → east) often means worsening weather. **Veering wind** (clockwise, for example south → west → northwest) usually means a front has passed and it's clearing.
- **Barometer:**

| Change over 3 hours | Means |
|---|---|
| Falling fast (> 0.06 inHg / 2 mb) | Storm approaching |
| Falling slowly | Rain likely, deteriorating |
| Steady | Little change |
| Rising slowly | Improving |
| Rising fast | Clearing, often windy and cooler |

- **Cold front:** a line of towering clouds/storms, sharp wind shift to northwest, temperature drop, then clearing and cooler, drier air.
- **Warm front:** thickening high clouds over a day, then long steady rain, then warmer and humid.

## Severe Weather Warning Signs
### Thunderstorms & Lightning
- **Count flash to bang:** seconds ÷ 5 = miles away. Under 30 seconds (6 miles), get inside a building or hard-top vehicle.
- **No place outdoors is safe.** Avoid hilltops, open fields, lone trees, water, metal fences.
- Groups: spread out 50+ ft apart so one strike can't hit everyone.
- Wait **30 minutes** after the last thunder.

### Tornadoes (the Ohio Valley gets them)
- Dark, often **greenish** sky; large hail; a loud continuous roar like a train; a low, rotating wall cloud; debris cloud at ground level; sudden calm after a storm's front.
- **Shelter:** basement or the lowest interior room without windows; cover your head. Get out of mobile homes and vehicles to sturdy shelter or a low ditch.

### Flash Floods
- Heavy rain upstream can flood a dry creek bed in minutes. **Never camp in a dry wash or right beside a creek.**
- Rising water, rumbling, debris, and muddy water are warnings. Climb to higher ground immediately.
- **Turn around, don't drown.** 6 in of moving water can knock you down; 12 in can float a car.

### Winter Storms
- Ring around the moon plus thickening clouds plus falling barometer plus wind backing to east/northeast in winter often means snow or ice.
- Freezing rain: temperatures just below freezing at the ground with warmer air aloft. Stay off roads.

## Keep a Weather Log
Write down cloud types, wind direction, barometer reading and what happened the next day in the [Logbook](/tools/logbook). After one season you'll be a better local forecaster than you'd expect.
