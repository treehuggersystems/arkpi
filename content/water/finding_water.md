# Finding & Collecting Water

**All found water must be purified** — see [Water Purification](/water/water_purification_methods). Clear and cold does *not* mean safe.

## Best to Worst Sources
1. **Rainwater** from a clean collection surface
2. **Springs** at the point they emerge from the ground
3. **Fast-moving streams and rivers**: upstream of farms, towns, roads
4. **Lakes and ponds**: collect away from the shore, below the surface
5. **Stagnant water**: last resort; filter heavily and boil

## Avoid
- Flood water: sewage and chemicals
- Water downstream of farms, mines, factories, landfills, roads
- Water with dead animals nearby, algae blooms (green/blue scum), oily sheen, or chemical smell
- Car radiators (antifreeze is deadly), waterbeds (algaecides), pools for drinking
- Seawater and urine to drink: they speed dehydration

## Rainwater Harvesting
- **1 inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft of roof ≈ 600 gallons.** (Roof area × inches × 0.6)
- Gutters → downspout → **first-flush diverter** (dumps the first dirty gallons) → screened barrel.
- Asphalt shingles are fine for non-drinking use. For drinking water, metal roofs or a clean tarp are better.
- Tarp catch: tie a tarp at an angle with a low corner over a container.
- Screen barrels against mosquitoes. Keep them dark to stop algae.
- **Always purify before drinking.**

## Finding Water in the Wild
- Follow **downhill** and green vegetation (willows, cattails, cottonwoods, sycamores grow near water).
- Animal trails converge toward water. Birds fly toward water at dawn and dusk.
- **Dig** at the outside bend of a dry stream bed, or a few feet back from a muddy pond edge (the water seeps in partly filtered).
- **Dew:** wipe grass at dawn with a cloth and wring it out. Slow, but works.
- **Transpiration bag:** clear plastic bag over a leafy, non-toxic branch in sun; ~½–1 cup per day.
- **Snow:** melt it before consuming (eating snow drops your core temperature). Add some water to the pot first so it doesn't scorch.

## Improvised Filter (clarifies only)
Removes silt and some taste. **It does NOT make water safe.** Still boil or chemically treat after.

Layers in a cut bottle or bucket with a hole, top to bottom:
1. Cloth (pre-filter)
2. Fine sand
3. **Crushed charcoal** from your fire (not briquettes): improves taste and odor
4. Fine sand
5. Small gravel
6. Coarse gravel
7. Cloth over the drain hole

Pour water through several times. Discard the first run.

## Settling & Pre-Filtering
- Let muddy water **settle** for hours, then pour off the top.
- Strain through a **bandana or coffee filter** before using a microfilter. It saves the filter from clogging.

## Priority
- **Rain is your best free source. Purify everything.**
