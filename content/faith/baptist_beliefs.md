# What Baptists Believe

Baptists share the historic Christian faith (one God in three persons, the full deity and humanity of Jesus Christ, His virgin birth, sinless life, atoning death, bodily resurrection and personal return, and salvation by grace through faith) along with a set of distinctive convictions. A common way to remember them is **B.A.P.T.I.S.T.S.**

## B — Biblical Authority
The Bible is God's inspired, inerrant Word and the final authority for faith and practice.

[[2 Timothy 3:16-17]]

## A — Autonomy of the Local Church
Each local congregation is self-governing under the lordship of Christ, cooperating voluntarily with other churches.

[[Colossians 1:18]]

## P — Priesthood of Every Believer
Every Christian has direct access to God through Christ, and every believer is called to serve and minister.

[[1 Peter 2:9]]

## T — Two Ordinances
**Believer's baptism by immersion** and **the Lord's Supper**, both symbolic acts of obedience and remembrance, not means of salvation.

[[Romans 6:3-4]]

[[1 Corinthians 11:26]]

## I — Individual Soul Liberty
Each person is accountable to God and free to choose and follow his conscience in matters of faith, without coercion.

[[Romans 14:12]]

## S — Saved, Baptized Church Membership
The church is made up of people who have personally trusted Christ and been baptized as believers.

[[Acts 2:41]]

## T — Two Offices
**Pastors** (also called elders or bishops/overseers) and **deacons**.

[[1 Timothy 3:1-2]]

[[1 Timothy 3:8]]

## S — Separation of Church and State
God established both church and government, but the state should neither control the church nor establish a religion. Baptists were historic champions of religious liberty.

[[Matthew 22:21]]

## Going Deeper
Many Baptist churches summarize their beliefs in a confession of faith, such as the **Baptist Faith & Message** (Southern Baptist) or the historic **1689 London Baptist Confession**. Ask your pastor for a printed copy of your church's confession and keep it with your ARK. Classic Baptist reading, all public domain, is in [Books & Media](/media): John Bunyan's *The Pilgrim's Progress* and C. H. Spurgeon's devotionals.
