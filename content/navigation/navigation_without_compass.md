# Finding Direction Without a Compass

## Shadow-Stick Method (most reliable, daytime)
1. Push a straight stick (~3 ft) upright in level ground.
2. Mark the tip of its shadow with a stone (**first mark = WEST**).
3. Wait **15–30 minutes**, mark the new shadow tip (**second mark = EAST**).
4. A line from first to second mark runs **west → east**. Stand with the first mark on your left and the second on your right: you're facing **north**.

Works anywhere, any time of day the sun casts a shadow. The longer you wait, the more accurate.

## Watch Method (analog watch or drawn clock)
**Northern Hemisphere:**
1. Hold the watch flat; point the **hour hand at the sun**.
2. Halfway between the hour hand and **12 o'clock** is **south**.
3. **During Daylight Saving Time, use 1 o'clock** instead of 12.

*Southern Hemisphere: point 12 at the sun; halfway between 12 and the hour hand is north.*
Least accurate near midday and at low latitudes.

## Sun Basics
- Rises roughly **east**, sets roughly **west**. Exactly east/west only near the equinoxes (Mar/Sep); in summer it rises NE and sets NW, in winter SE/SW.
- At **solar noon** the sun is due **south** (in the US) and shadows point **due north**. Get solar noon from the [Sun & Moon tool](/tools/sun).

## Night: The North Star (Polaris)
1. Find the **Big Dipper**.
2. The two stars at the outer edge of the "cup" are the **pointer stars**.
3. Follow the line from the bottom pointer through the top pointer **about 5×** their distance.
4. That moderately bright star is **Polaris**, the end of the Little Dipper's handle. **It's within 1° of true north.**
- Can't see the Dipper? **Cassiopeia** (a "W") sits on the opposite side of Polaris; Polaris is off the middle of the W.
- Polaris's height above the horizon ≈ your **latitude**.

## Orion
- **Orion's Belt** (three stars in a row) rises almost **due east** and sets almost **due west**.
- The **sword** hanging from the belt points roughly **south**.

## The Moon
- If the moon rises **before the sun has set**, the lit side faces **west**.
- If the moon rises **after midnight**, the lit side faces **east**.
- **Crescent trick:** draw a line connecting the two horns and extend it down to the horizon: that's roughly **south** (Northern Hemisphere).

## Natural Signs: Use With Caution
| Sign | Reliability |
|---|---|
| **Moss on the north side of trees** | **Unreliable.** Moss grows wherever it's damp and shady |
| Snow melts faster on south-facing slopes | Fairly reliable in open terrain |
| Trees fuller / more branches on the south side | Somewhat, in open areas |
| Prevailing winds (mostly W→E in the US) bend lone trees | Local knowledge needed |
| Satellite TV dishes in the continental US point **south** (SSW–SSE) | **Very reliable** in towns |
| Churches traditionally have altars at the east end | Old churches, often |

## Staying Found
- Before you leave, **note a big baseline**: "the river is south of me, the highway is east."
- If lost: **STOP** — **S**it, **T**hink, **O**bserve, **P**lan. Don't wander. Staying put makes you easier to find if anyone is looking.
